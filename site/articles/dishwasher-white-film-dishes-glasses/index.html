Why Is My Dishwasher Leaving White Film on Dishes and Glasses?

White dishwasher residue and permanent glass etching can look similar. A simple vinegar test separates a cleanable deposit from irreversible surface damage.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • White dishwasher residue and permanent glass etching can look similar. A simple vinegar test separates a cleanable deposit from irreversible surface damage.
  • Use vinegar to determine whether the cloudiness is removable film or permanent etching.

Test one affected glass.

Soak or wipe a small area with plain white vinegar, then rinse it.

  • Cloudiness disappears: The residue is probably removable mineral or detergent film.
  • Cloudiness remains: The glass may be permanently etched.

Whirlpool and GE both recommend a vinegar screen to distinguish removable deposits from permanent etching.

Hard water commonly causes removable film

Hard water contains dissolved minerals, especially calcium and magnesium.

During washing and drying, those minerals can remain on:

  • Glasses
  • Flatware
  • Plastic
  • Interior walls
  • Heating element
  • Spray arms

GE identifies hard-water minerals and interactions with phosphate-free detergent as common causes of white or milky dishwasher film.

Check the home’s water hardness through:

  • Utility water-quality report
  • Water-softener setting
  • Test strip
  • Laboratory result
  • Appliance setup information

Do not guess from taste alone.

Use detergent according to water hardness

More detergent is not always better.

Hard water

May require:

  • Correct detergent dose
  • Detergent formulated for hard water
  • Rinse aid
  • Dishwasher salt on models with an internal softener
  • Periodic descaling

Soft water

Usually needs less detergent.

GE says excessive detergent in soft water can contribute to irreversible glass etching.

Follow the dishwasher and detergent instructions together.

A detergent pod has one fixed amount, which may be excessive for a small load or very soft water and insufficient for another machine operating with hard water and heavy soil.

Check the rinse-aid dispenser

Rinse aid helps water spread into a thinner sheet rather than forming droplets that dry into spots.

Bosch describes rinse aid as a drying aid that reduces water marks and mineral spotting.

Check:

  • Dispenser filled
  • Cap installed
  • Adjustment level
  • No leaking dispenser
  • Product compatible with the dishwasher

Increase the setting gradually when spotting persists.

Reduce it when dishes develop bluish film, excessive foam, or manufacturer-identified overdispensing symptoms.

Clean the filter

A dirty filter can recirculate:

  • Food particles
  • Detergent residue
  • Grease
  • Mineral debris

Remove and clean it according to the manual.

Whirlpool identifies filter blockage as one cause of residue and inadequate cleaning.

Do not operate the dishwasher without its filter.

Check spray-arm movement

Before starting a cycle:

  1. Load the racks.
  2. Rotate every spray arm by hand.
  3. Confirm no pan, utensil, or cutting board blocks it.
  4. Check visible holes for debris.
  5. Confirm the detergent door can open.

Poor circulation can leave detergent and mineral deposits because water does not reach every surface or rinse them adequately.

Do not enlarge spray holes with a drill or metal tool.

Load glasses at an angle

Position cups and glasses so water can drain.

Avoid:

  • Deep horizontal surfaces
  • Nested bowls
  • Glasses touching
  • Tall utensils blocking the upper arm
  • Large boards facing the detergent dispenser

Pooled water evaporates and leaves whatever was dissolved in it.

Descale only as directed

A dishwasher exposed to hard water may need a manufacturer-approved descaling cycle.

Bosch recommends model-compatible descaling products for mineral buildup and distinguishes descaling from ordinary filter cleaning.

Do not combine:

  • Descaler
  • Chlorine bleach
  • Dishwasher detergent
  • Vinegar
  • Another acid
  • Drain cleaner

Run one approved product through one documented procedure.

Permanent glass etching

Etching is microscopic surface damage.

Possible contributors include:

  • Excess detergent
  • Very soft water
  • Excessively high wash temperature
  • Repeated harsh cycles
  • Glass not suitable for dishwasher use

GE says etched glass remains cloudy after vinegar testing because the surface itself has been damaged.

Etching cannot be washed off.

Prevent further damage by:

  • Reducing detergent
  • Using a gentler cycle
  • Checking water-softener settings
  • Hand-washing delicate glass

Do not use hand dish soap

Ordinary hand dishwashing liquid creates excessive foam in a dishwasher.

Foam can:

  • Leak through the door
  • Interfere with spraying
  • Trigger errors
  • Leave residue
  • Damage flooring

Use only automatic-dishwasher detergent.

Bottom line

Use vinegar to determine whether the cloudiness is removable film or permanent etching.

For removable film, address water hardness, detergent dose, rinse aid, filter condition, spray-arm movement, and descaling. For etched glass, adjust the process before additional glassware receives the same permanent decorative treatment.
