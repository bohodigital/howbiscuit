How to Choose a Battery-Backup Sump Pump

A backup pump protects against power loss, primary-pump failure, stuck floats, and sometimes inflow beyond the primary pump’s capacity. Its advertised zero-head flow is not the flow available at your discharge height.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • A backup pump protects against power loss, primary-pump failure, stuck floats, and sometimes inflow beyond the primary pump’s capacity. Its advertised zero-head flow is not the flow available at your discharge height.
  • Choose from pump-curve capacity at actual head, independent float operation, battery compatibility, realistic runtime, charger quality, alarms, and installation.

Start with the existing sump system.

Record:

  • Basin diameter and depth
  • Primary pump model
  • Primary pump flow at actual head
  • Vertical rise
  • Pipe diameter
  • Check valve
  • Power circuit
  • Typical water inflow
  • Longest expected outage
  • Available battery location

FEMA recommends backup power for sump pumps because flood events often coincide with electrical outages.

Understand what the backup covers

A battery backup can activate when:

  • Utility power fails
  • Primary pump fails
  • Primary float sticks
  • Water rises above the backup float
  • Primary pump cannot keep up

Backup systems are secondary devices rather than replacements for a correctly sized and maintained primary pump. Zoeller and Liberty both describe their DC systems as backups used alongside an AC primary pump.

Compare flow at actual head

Manufacturers commonly publish flow at:

  • 0 feet
  • 5 feet
  • 10 feet
  • Another head height

Head includes vertical lift and system resistance.

A unit advertised at 2,600 gallons per hour at zero head may deliver substantially less at a 10-foot lift. Basement Watchdog, for example, lists one model at 2,600 gallons per hour at zero feet and 1,850 gallons per hour at 10 feet.

Compare pump curves, not the largest number on the box.

Measure the discharge path

Record:

  • Vertical distance from pump outlet to highest pipe point
  • Horizontal distance
  • Elbows
  • Pipe diameter
  • Check valves
  • Exterior termination
  • Freeze exposure

A restrictive or partially blocked discharge reduces real flow.

Do not route backup discharge into:

  • Sanitary sewer where prohibited
  • Foundation area
  • Frozen outlet
  • Location that returns water to the sump

Use a plumber where code or system design is uncertain.

Independent pump versus inverter

Dedicated DC backup pump

Uses a separate 12-volt pump, float, controller, battery, and charger.

Advantages:

  • Independent pump
  • Lower DC power demand
  • Continues if the primary motor fails

Battery inverter for the primary pump

Converts battery power to AC and runs an approved primary pump.

Advantages:

  • Can preserve the main pump’s capacity
  • May support sewage or larger pumps where designed

Tradeoffs:

  • Larger battery demand
  • Compatibility limits
  • More complex power electronics

Liberty sells both dedicated DC backup pumps and inverter systems intended for specified pump loads.

Choose the correct battery

Common options include:

  • Flooded deep-cycle lead-acid
  • AGM standby battery
  • Manufacturer-specific battery

Compare:

  • Required group size
  • Amp-hour capacity
  • Ventilation
  • Maintenance
  • Terminal type
  • Charger compatibility
  • Expected runtime
  • Replacement availability

Do not substitute an automotive starting battery merely because it is 12 volts.

Manufacturers often specify deep-cycle or dedicated standby batteries engineered for repeated backup operation.

Runtime claims require context

Runtime depends on:

  • Water inflow
  • Pump duty cycle
  • Discharge height
  • Battery capacity
  • Battery age
  • Temperature
  • Charger condition

A claim that a system runs “for days” usually means intermittent operation, not uninterrupted pumping at maximum load.

FEMA warns that battery backups may last only hours during long-duration events and may need another backup-power plan.

Require alarms and monitoring

Useful alerts include:

  • Power failure
  • Backup pump activation
  • Low battery
  • Loose battery connection
  • Charger failure
  • High water
  • Failed float
  • Remote notification

An internet alert is useful only when:

  • Router has backup power
  • Internet service remains available
  • Account is active
  • Notification reaches a monitored device

Keep a local audible alarm.

Basin and float clearance

The backup float must move independently.

Check:

  • Basin diameter
  • Pump spacing
  • Float travel
  • Primary intake height
  • Backup intake height
  • Check-valve placement

Zoeller’s instructions require the backup intake above the primary intake and direct users to test the operational float.

A backup float trapped against the pit wall is a decorative sensor.

Professional installation

Use a qualified installer when work involves:

  • New discharge piping
  • Electrical outlet
  • Battery ventilation
  • Sewage
  • Shared building system
  • Sealed basin
  • Water-powered backup
  • Complex check valves
  • Local permit or code

Water-powered backup systems require adequate municipal water pressure and do not work during a power outage on a private well unless the well itself remains powered.

Bottom line

Choose from pump-curve capacity at actual head, independent float operation, battery compatibility, realistic runtime, charger quality, alarms, and installation.

A backup sump pump is not purchased by horsepower vibes. It is a small emergency pumping system whose value depends on whether every component works on the wettest night of the year.
