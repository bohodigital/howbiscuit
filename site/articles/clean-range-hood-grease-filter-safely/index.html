How to Clean a Range Hood Grease Filter Safely

Metal mesh and baffle filters collect airborne grease and are commonly washable. Recirculating charcoal filters capture odors and usually require replacement rather than cleaning.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Metal mesh and baffle filters collect airborne grease and are commonly washable. Recirculating charcoal filters capture odors and usually require replacement rather than cleaning.
  • Wash approved metal grease filters, replace charcoal filters, keep liquid away from electrical parts, and reinstall only when dry.

Turn the hood and cooktop off. Wait for every surface to cool.

Remove the grease filter using the exact model instructions, then clean it with warm water and detergent or in a dishwasher only when the manufacturer permits that method.

Identify the filter type

Metal grease filter

May use aluminum mesh, stainless mesh, or baffle construction. It captures airborne grease and is commonly washable.

Charcoal or carbon filter

Used in many recirculating hoods to reduce odor. It is usually not washable and must be replaced.

Combination system

Some hoods use a washable metal filter plus a separate charcoal insert.

Do not put the charcoal layer into dishwater because it happened to be physically attached to a washable frame.

Find the model manual

Record:

  • Manufacturer
  • Model
  • Filter part number
  • Removal direction
  • Dishwasher approval
  • Replacement interval
  • Charcoal-filter requirement

Filters may release through a spring latch, pull tab, sliding clip, or retaining frame.

Support the filter while releasing it. A grease-coated metal filter is surprisingly committed to landing corner-first on either the glass cooktop or a human foot.

Prepare the area

Use:

  • Gloves
  • Sink stopper or basin
  • Mild dish detergent
  • Soft nonmetal brush
  • Clean towels

Protect the sink from sharp corners.

Do not clean the filter while burners are hot, the hood fan is running, grease is dripping near ignition sources, or electrical components are wet.

Soak a washable filter

When the manual permits hand washing:

  1. Fill a basin with warm water.
  2. Add ordinary dish detergent.
  3. Submerge the filter.
  4. Allow grease to soften.
  5. Swirl gently.
  6. Brush stubborn areas with a soft brush.
  7. Rinse thoroughly.
  8. Let it dry completely.

Avoid ammonia and harsh alkaline cleaners unless the manufacturer expressly permits them. They can discolor or damage aluminum and other materials.

Do not mix degreasers, bleach, ammonia, acids, or unidentified products.

Dishwasher cleaning

Some manufacturers permit dishwasher cleaning.

Check:

  • Filter material
  • Rack placement
  • Detergent restrictions
  • Wash temperature
  • Whether discoloration is expected

Metal filters can discolor in a dishwasher without necessarily losing function, but the exact manufacturer guidance controls.

Do not place a filter in the dishwasher merely because it fits.

Clean accessible hood surfaces

With the hood off and cool:

  • Wipe the filter frame.
  • Remove grease from the lower hood surface.
  • Clean around clips.
  • Follow finish-specific instructions.

Apply cleaner to a cloth rather than spraying upward into the motor, switches, lights, wiring, fan, or control board.

Do not reach into the blower wheel.

Reinstall only when dry

A wet filter can drip into electrical components or onto the cooktop.

Before reinstalling:

  1. Confirm complete dryness.
  2. Check for torn mesh.
  3. Confirm the frame is not bent.
  4. Insert it in the documented direction.
  5. Engage every latch.
  6. Pull gently to verify retention.
  7. Run the fan briefly.

Replace damaged filters with the correct part.

How often should it be cleaned?

Use the manual and actual cooking conditions.

Clean more often with:

  • Frying
  • High-heat searing
  • Frequent cooking
  • Heavy oil use
  • Visible buildup
  • Persistent odor

A lightly used hood may need less frequent attention. A filter carrying a glossy brown layer has already submitted its maintenance request in a reasonably clear format.

Call for service when

  • Grease reached the motor
  • Fan vibrates
  • Blower grinds
  • Hood trips the breaker
  • Controls are damaged
  • Ductwork contains heavy grease
  • Filter cannot be removed safely
  • Commercial cooking is involved

Household cleaning does not replace commercial exhaust-system inspection and fire-code maintenance.

Bottom line

Wash approved metal grease filters, replace charcoal filters, keep liquid away from electrical parts, and reinstall only when dry.

The filter exists so grease does not become a permanent interior finish for the blower and duct. Cleaning it before that transition is cheaper.
