Why Does My Toilet Gurgle When Another Drain Runs?

Toilet gurgling occurs when air or wastewater moves through the drainage system abnormally. One isolated sound may be minor, but multiple slow drains or sewage movement can precede a backup.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 3 min read Researched Not hands-on tested

What to know

  • Toilet gurgling occurs when air or wastewater moves through the drainage system abnormally. One isolated sound may be minor, but multiple slow drains or sewage movement can precede a backup.
  • One isolated gurgle may be a local obstruction or temporary pressure event.

Stop using water when toilet gurgling occurs with:

  • Several slow drains
  • Water rising in a shower or floor drain
  • Sewage odor
  • Toilet backup
  • Gurgling from multiple fixtures

Multiple slow drains, cross-fixture backup, foul odor, and gurgling are warning signs of a restricted main sewer line.

Why a toilet gurgles

A drainage system moves both wastewater and air.

Plumbing vents admit and release air so traps and pipes can drain without destructive pressure differences.

A gurgle means air is being pulled or pushed through water in the toilet bowl or another trap.

Possible causes include:

  • Local toilet obstruction
  • Branch-drain blockage
  • Main sewer restriction
  • Blocked plumbing vent
  • Septic-system problem
  • Municipal sewer cleaning
  • Building-wide drainage issue

The sound alone cannot identify which one.

Test the pattern without creating a backup

Observe whether gurgling occurs when:

  • Toilet flushes
  • Sink drains
  • Bathtub drains
  • Washing machine pumps out
  • Dishwasher drains
  • Another unit uses water

Do not repeatedly flush a toilet that is already rising.

Do not run several fixtures to “stress test” a suspected main-line clog. The next result may be sewage on the floor, which is impressively conclusive and entirely avoidable.

One toilet only

When only one toilet gurgles and other drains remain normal, possible causes include:

  • Partial toilet-trap obstruction
  • Object in the toilet
  • Restricted nearby branch
  • Pressure-assisted operation issue

Use a flange-style toilet plunger only when there is no sign of a main-line backup and the bowl level remains controllable.

Do not use chemical drain opener in a toilet. It may not clear the obstruction and can create corrosive splash exposure for whoever later removes the fixture.

Multiple fixtures point farther downstream

When draining one fixture affects another, the obstruction may lie in a shared branch or building sewer.

Stop:

  • Laundry
  • Dishwasher
  • Showering
  • Toilet flushing
  • Sink use

Call the sewer utility or plumber according to local responsibility.

Municipal sewer cleaning can cause temporary bubbling

High-pressure sewer cleaning can move air through connected building drains.

Utility jetting can produce temporary toilet or drain bubbling, gurgling, and occasional splashing.

Check:

  • Utility notices
  • Work trucks nearby
  • Neighbor reports
  • Whether the sound began during scheduled cleaning

Keep toilet lids closed during nearby jetting.

Persistent backup or sewage flow still requires reporting.

Septic systems

For a private septic system, gurgling with slow drains or backup can involve:

  • Full tank
  • Blocked inlet or outlet
  • Clogged effluent filter
  • Saturated drainfield
  • Damaged piping

Stop water use and contact a licensed septic professional.

Do not open or enter a septic tank. Toxic gases and confined-space hazards can kill.

Blocked vent

A blocked roof vent can contribute to gurgling, slow drainage, trap disturbance, and sewer odor.

Possible blockage includes leaves, nests, ice, or construction debris.

Roof access is not an ordinary plumbing test.

Use a plumber, particularly when the roof is steep, wet, icy, or near electrical service.

Sewage backup response

When wastewater enters the home:

  1. Stop all water use.
  2. Keep children and pets away.
  3. Avoid electrical equipment in wet areas.
  4. Call the sewer utility or plumber.
  5. Photograph damage safely.
  6. Contact the insurer or landlord.
  7. Use qualified cleanup for contaminated material.

Bottom line

One isolated gurgle may be a local obstruction or temporary pressure event.

Gurgling across several fixtures, slow drainage, sewage odor, or cross-fixture water movement can signal a main sewer or septic problem. Stop adding water before the house performs its own deeply unwanted demonstration.
