Power Outage Food Safety Chart: What to Keep and What to Throw Away

Use this chart after a household power outage.

Use this chart after a household power outage.

The basic rules are:

  • Refrigerator: about 4 hours if unopened
  • Full freezer: about 48 hours if unopened
  • Half-full freezer: about 24 hours if unopened
  • Refreeze frozen food that still has ice crystals or is 40°F or below
  • Discard perishable food that was above 40°F for more than 2 hours
  • Use a 1-hour limit for perishable food outside temperature control when the air temperature is above 90°F
  • Never taste food to decide whether it is safe[1][2][3]

Common refrigerated foods

Discard after unsafe warming

  • Meat
  • Poultry
  • Seafood
  • Milk
  • Eggs
  • Soft cheese
  • Deli meat
  • Leftovers
  • Pizza
  • Cooked rice and pasta
  • Cooked vegetables
  • Cut melon
  • Packaged cut greens
  • Cream-filled pastries
  • Custard or cream pies

May remain safe under USDA guidance

  • Hard cheese
  • Processed cheese
  • Butter and margarine
  • Whole fruits and vegetables
  • Bread and rolls
  • Fruit pies
  • Peanut butter
  • Jelly
  • Mustard
  • Ketchup
  • Pickles
  • Relish
  • Flour and nuts

Use the official item-specific chart for foods not listed here.

During the outage

  • Keep doors closed.
  • Write down the outage start time.
  • Prepare a cooler if the outage will exceed four hours.
  • Add ice or gel packs.
  • Keep cooler food at 40°F or below.
  • Group frozen food together.
  • Use dry ice only with proper gloves, ventilation, and handling.
  • Do not put food outside in snow.
  • Do not taste questionable food.

When power returns

  1. Check the appliance thermometer.
  2. Check the most exposed freezer packages.
  3. Look for ice crystals.
  4. Measure individual food when needed.
  5. Separate leaking raw foods.
  6. Keep, refreeze, cook, or discard each item.
  7. Clean contaminated surfaces.
  8. Record what was discarded for insurance or assistance documentation when relevant.

Refrigerator and freezer thermometer targets

  • Refrigerator: 40°F or below
  • Freezer: 0°F or below during normal operation
  • Outage refreezing threshold: 40°F or below, or ice crystals remain

Keep one appliance thermometer in each compartment.

Important limitations

The time estimates assume:

  • The appliance was cold before the outage
  • Doors stayed closed
  • The appliance was functioning normally
  • Food was not already left out
  • No floodwater or chemical contamination occurred

This chart does not override:

  • Product labels
  • A public-health recall
  • Flood contamination guidance
  • Instructions for medically necessary foods or medications
  • Advice from a local health department during a specific emergency

Help

For food-safety questions, FoodSafety.gov directs consumers to the USDA Meat and Poultry Hotline at 1-888-674-6854 and Ask USDA.[1]

Seek medical advice if someone develops symptoms of foodborne illness, especially if they are pregnant, older, very young, immunocompromised, or seriously ill.
