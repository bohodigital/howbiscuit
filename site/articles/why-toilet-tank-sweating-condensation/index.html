Why Is My Toilet Tank Sweating?

A toilet tank sweats when humid bathroom air contacts porcelain cooled below the air’s dew point by cold refill water. Lower the room humidity, improve exhaust, repair a running toilet, and confirm the water is not actually coming from a plumbing leak.

Updated Aug 3, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Dry the tank and fittings first so you can distinguish broad condensation from a localized leak.
  • Run a working bathroom exhaust fan during bathing and after the room fills with steam.
  • A leaking flapper can keep the tank cold by causing repeated refills.
  • A manufacturer-approved lined tank or professionally installed anti-sweat valve can help persistent cases.

The direct answer

A toilet tank “sweats” when humid air touches cold porcelain and water vapor condenses into liquid.

The tank becomes cold because it repeatedly fills with cold supply water. Condensation becomes more likely during humid weather, after steamy showers, with poor bathroom ventilation, or when a running toilet refills frequently.

EPA explains that moisture condenses when warm humid air contacts a cooler surface and recommends reducing indoor humidity and drying wet materials promptly. [1]

Confirm that it is condensation

Dry the tank, fittings, supply line, and floor completely. Then observe where water first returns.

Condensation usually appears as fine droplets over a broad exterior area and becomes worse after showers or repeated flushing.

A plumbing leak is more likely when water begins at the supply valve, flexible connector, fill-valve shank, tank bolts, tank-to-bowl gasket, flush valve, or a crack in the porcelain.

Use dry tissue around each connection to locate a small leak.

Do not aggressively tighten fittings attached to porcelain. Overtightening can crack the tank.

Measure bathroom humidity

Use a hygrometer to record relative humidity before and after showering, during the condensation, and in an adjacent room.

EPA generally recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60% and ideally around 30% to 50% where practical. [1]

The exact level required to prevent sweating depends on tank temperature and room temperature.

Use the bathroom exhaust fan

Run the fan during bathing and afterward long enough to remove visible moisture.

Check that:

  • The fan actually draws air.
  • The grille is clean.
  • The duct discharges outdoors.
  • The exterior damper opens.
  • The bathroom door permits replacement air.
  • The fan is appropriately sized.

A fan that makes impressive noise while moving little air remains mostly a decoration.

Open a window only when outdoor humidity is lower and doing so is safe.

Dry the tank and floor

Do not let water remain on flooring, baseboard, wall, toilet hardware, supply valves, or cabinetry.

Persistent condensation can contribute to mold, swollen flooring, staining, corrosion, damaged trim, and hidden moisture beneath the toilet.

EPA recommends fixing the moisture source and drying wet materials promptly. [1]

A tray may protect the floor temporarily, but it does not stop condensation.

Repair unnecessary refilling

A leaking flapper or fill valve can cause the toilet to refill repeatedly, keeping the tank colder than necessary.

Listen for intermittent refilling, running water, rippling bowl water, or the fill valve cycling when nobody flushed.

Repairing a running toilet can reduce both water waste and condensation. Use parts approved for the exact model.

Lined toilet tanks

A tank liner reduces heat transfer between cold tank water and the exterior porcelain.

American Standard sells factory-lined tanks specifically to reduce exterior condensation in humid environments. [2]

Options can include a factory-lined replacement tank, a toilet designed for humid conditions, or a manufacturer-approved internal liner.

Do not install a generic liner that interferes with the float, fill valve, flush valve, chain, overflow tube, water level, or warranty.

Anti-sweat valves

An anti-sweat valve mixes a controlled amount of warm water with the cold toilet supply so the tank surface does not become as cold.

Installation can require a hot-water connection, shutoff valves, backflow consideration, code compliance, and correct adjustment.

Use a qualified plumber when appropriate. Incorrect installation can waste hot water, create leaks, or produce an unsuitable tank temperature.

Dehumidification

A dehumidifier may help when the entire room, basement, or home remains humid.

Use safe electrical connection and drainage, keep windows closed when required, and address leaks and ventilation defects rather than making the appliance a permanent employee assigned to compensate for them.

Renters

Report repeated condensation or suspected leakage in writing. Include photographs, humidity readings, whether it follows showers or flushing, whether the toilet refills by itself, floor damage, and the first visible water location.

Do not modify the water supply or install a mixing valve without permission.

Bottom line

Toilet-tank sweating is usually condensation caused by cold water and humid air.

Lower bathroom humidity, improve exhaust, repair a running toilet, and keep the floor dry. For persistent cases, consider a manufacturer-approved lined tank or professionally installed anti-sweat valve after confirming that the water is not actually coming from a leak.

Limits and cautions

  • The humidity level that causes condensation depends on room temperature and tank-water temperature.
  • Tank liners and anti-sweat valves must be compatible with the toilet and plumbing installation.
  • Condensation can coexist with a supply, bolt, gasket, or tank leak.
