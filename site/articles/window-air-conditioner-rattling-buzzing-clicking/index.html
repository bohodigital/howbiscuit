Why Is My Window Air Conditioner Rattling, Buzzing, Clicking, or Pinging?

Some ticking and pinging from a window air conditioner is normal because the rear fan throws condensate against the condenser. Violent rattling, electrical buzzing, grinding, or a loose window installation is not.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested

What to know

  • Some ticking and pinging from a window air conditioner is normal because the rear fan throws condensate against the condenser. Violent rattling, electrical buzzing, grinding, or a loose window installation is not.
  • Light pinging or splashing can be normal condensate management.

Turn the unit off and listen for whether the noise stops immediately.

Then identify:

  • Noise type
  • Exact location
  • Fan-only versus cooling mode
  • Low versus high fan speed
  • Whether the window frame vibrates
  • Whether the noise began after installation or cleaning

GE identifies loose grilles, movable louvers, and improper window installation as common causes of rattling. It also identifies some ticking or pinging from the slinger ring as normal.

Pinging or splashing can be normal

Many window air conditioners retain some condensate in the outdoor base pan.

A ring attached to the outdoor fan can throw that water against the condenser coil. This can improve heat transfer and help remove water through evaporation.

The result may sound like:

  • Pinging
  • Light ticking
  • Splashing
  • Water movement

GE describes this slinger-ring sound as normal operation on supported models.

Do not drill a drain hole into a unit whose design intentionally retains water.

Check the installation

Turn the unit off and inspect from indoors.

Check:

  • Side panels secured
  • Lower sash firmly seated
  • Brackets installed
  • Cabinet resting as specified
  • Foam seals positioned correctly
  • No loose decorative trim
  • No contact with blinds or curtains

GE says an improperly secured window installation can vibrate against the sill and create rattling.

Do not loosen the unit without another person supporting it where the manufacturer requires two-person handling.

Press lightly on the front grille

With the unit off, press gently on:

  • Front grille
  • Filter cover
  • Directional louvers
  • Control-panel trim

A loose grille or louver can buzz or rattle when airflow or compressor vibration begins. GE lists loose or broken front and directional components among common noise sources.

Use only the model’s approved clips or screws.

Do not wedge paper, coins, or foam into an air path.

Compare fan-only and cooling modes

Run Fan only briefly.

  • Noise in fan-only mode suggests the blower, grille, filter, louver, or cabinet.
  • Noise only when cooling begins may involve the compressor, refrigerant flow, outdoor fan, or water movement.

A compressor often makes a click or brief hum when starting.

Stop the unit when it:

  • Hums without starting
  • Repeatedly clicks every few seconds
  • Trips the breaker
  • Smells burned
  • Produces smoke
  • Makes a violent mechanical knock

Check the filter

A dirty or incorrectly seated filter can:

  • Flutter
  • Restrict airflow
  • Pull against its frame
  • Increase fan noise

Remove and clean it according to the manual.

Reinstall it fully dry and correctly oriented.

Do not operate the unit without the filter except where a specific diagnostic instruction permits a very brief test.

Inspect for visible fan interference

Disconnect power.

Use a flashlight through the approved openings.

Look for:

  • Leaf
  • Plastic bag
  • Loose insulation
  • Broken louver
  • Dislodged filter
  • Insect debris
  • Bent visible guard

Do not reach toward the fan or bend condenser fins.

Do not remove the chassis from the sleeve unless the manual identifies that as an owner procedure.

Electrical buzzing

A faint operating hum can be normal.

Stop and arrange service for buzzing associated with:

  • Hot plug
  • Hot receptacle
  • Melted plastic
  • Flickering power
  • Repeated breaker trip
  • Burning odor
  • Failure to start

Window air conditioners are high-load appliances and often require direct connection to a properly rated receptacle.

Do not use an extension cord unless the exact manufacturer explicitly approves one with stated specifications.

Grinding or scraping

Grinding suggests mechanical contact or bearing trouble.

Possible sources include:

  • Fan wheel contacting housing
  • Loose motor mount
  • Damaged fan blade
  • Motor bearing
  • Foreign object

Turn the unit off.

Continued operation can convert a small mechanical problem into motor or electrical damage.

Bottom line

Light pinging or splashing can be normal condensate management.

Rattling often comes from installation, grille, louvers, or filter. Grinding, violent vibration, electrical buzzing, smoke, or repeated starting failure requires service.

The useful question is not whether the air conditioner makes noise. It is whether the noise belongs to water and airflow or to something preparing to detach itself.
