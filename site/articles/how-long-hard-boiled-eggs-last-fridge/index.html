How Long Do Hard-Boiled Eggs Last in the Fridge?

Refrigerate hard-boiled eggs within two hours after cooking and use them within one week. Keep the refrigerator at 40°F or below and discard eggs whose time or temperature history is unsafe or unknown.

Updated Aug 2, 2026 4 min read Researched Testing not applicable

What to know

  • The one-week refrigerated limit applies to peeled and unpeeled hard-boiled eggs.
  • Store eggs in the main refrigerator compartment rather than the temperature-variable door.
  • For transport, keep eggs at 40°F or below with ice or frozen gel packs.

Hard-boiled eggs should be refrigerated within two hours after cooking and used within one week.

USDA lists hard-cooked eggs at one week under refrigeration and advises keeping the refrigerator at 40°F or below. [1] [2]

The one-week period applies whether the eggs remain in their shells or are peeled.

Cool and refrigerate promptly

After cooking, cool the eggs promptly according to the cooking method, dry the shells or peeled surfaces, place them in a clean covered container, refrigerate within two hours, and mark the cooking date.

USDA specifically says hard-cooked eggs should be refrigerated within two hours. [1]

Do not leave a large batch on the counter until everyone remembers it exists several hours later.

Should you peel them before storing?

Either method can work.

Storing in the shell

The shell reduces handling, offers some protection from drying, and makes the food easy to identify as a whole egg.

Storing peeled

Peeled eggs are faster to use for lunches and meal preparation. Keep them in a covered container so they do not dry out or absorb refrigerator odors. Handle them with clean hands and utensils.

The USDA storage limit remains one week for hard-cooked eggs. [1]

Why do cooked eggs spoil faster than raw shell eggs?

Hard cooking changes the egg and can leave moisture beneath the shell. USDA explains that this moisture creates a more favorable environment for bacterial growth and spoilage than exists in an intact refrigerated raw egg. [1]

Raw eggs in their shells may remain refrigerated for several weeks, but that longer raw-egg period should not be applied after cooking.

Keep them out of the refrigerator door

Store eggs in the main refrigerator compartment where temperature is more stable, not in a frequently opened door bin.

USDA recommends keeping eggs in the coldest part of the refrigerator rather than the door. [1]

Use an appliance thermometer if the refrigerator’s actual temperature is uncertain.

Transporting hard-boiled eggs

For lunches, picnics, or travel:

  • Pack eggs with enough ice or frozen gel packs to keep them at 40°F or below.
  • Use an insulated container.
  • Keep the cooler out of direct sunlight.
  • Return unused eggs to refrigeration only if they remained safely cold.
  • Discard eggs left at room temperature beyond safe limits.

The ordinary two-hour limit becomes one hour when ambient temperature is above 90°F under USDA’s general guidance for perishable food. [2]

Can hard-boiled eggs be frozen?

USDA lists hard-cooked eggs as unsuitable for freezing because their quality deteriorates. [1]

The whites tend to become tough or watery after thawing. A prepared egg dish may freeze more successfully when an official storage chart or tested recipe supports it.

What does a green ring mean?

A green or gray ring around the yolk usually results from a reaction involving sulfur and iron during extended cooking or slow cooling. It is a quality issue rather than automatic evidence that the egg has spoiled.

Storage time and temperature still determine whether it should be eaten.

When to throw an egg away

Discard it when more than seven days have passed since cooking, it remained unrefrigerated beyond the safe time, the refrigerator was above 40°F for an unsafe period, visible mold or unusual slime is present, the shell or container was contaminated, or you cannot determine how long it has been stored.

Odor alone cannot prove food is safe. When the storage history is unknown, discard the egg.

What about dyed or hidden eggs?

Decorative eggs used for games or displays may encounter dirt, grass, animals, extensive handling, sunlight, and unsafe temperatures.

Do not eat eggs hidden outdoors or left unrefrigerated beyond safe limits. Use separate nonfood eggs for extended displays.

Bottom line

Refrigerate hard-boiled eggs within two hours, keep them at 40°F or below, and use them within one week.

Label the cooking date instead of attempting to reconstruct the breakfast timeline from memory on day nine.

Limits and cautions

  • Appearance and odor cannot prove an egg is safe.
  • Storage recommendations assume ordinary home handling and a refrigerator maintained at 40°F or below.
