Why Is My Shower Water Pressure Low?

Start by determining whether the problem affects one shower, all fixtures, only hot water, or the entire property.

Low shower output is often caused by a restricted showerhead or valve. Whole-home or sudden changes can instead involve a shutoff valve, pressure regulator, leak, service line, neighborhood work, or utility problem.

What to know

  • Pressure and flow are related but are not the same measurement.
  • One weak shower usually points to a local restriction.
  • Hot-only weakness points toward the hot-water system or mixing valve.
  • A sudden whole-home drop may require a utility or plumber.
  • Do not drill out or remove flow-control components.

Compare fixtures before disassembly

Test the affected shower on cold, the same shower on hot, a nearby sink on cold and hot, another shower or faucet, and an exterior hose connection when safe and available.

The pattern matters:

  • Only one shower is weak: Check the showerhead, hose, diverter, cartridge, or local valve.
  • Every hot fixture is weak: Check the water heater, hot-water valve, piping, or a sediment-related restriction.
  • Hot and cold are weak throughout: The problem may involve the building supply, shutoff, regulator, leak, meter, or utility.
  • The problem began suddenly: An active leak, valve movement, utility work, or a water-main break deserves priority.

Municipal utilities recommend comparing multiple fixtures and hot-versus-cold behavior because those observations help separate a fixture problem from a water-heater or building-supply problem. [1]

Measure flow without calling it pressure

A container-and-timer test measures flow rate: Q = 60V / t, where Q is gallons per minute, V is collected gallons, and t is collection time in seconds.

If half a gallon collects in 20 seconds, the flow rate is 1.5 gallons per minute.

This does not measure static or dynamic pressure in pounds per square inch. It simply gives a repeatable flow comparison. Keep the floor dry and avoid handling containers in dangerously hot water.

Clean the showerhead correctly

Mineral scale and debris can restrict the spray openings or inlet screen.

Follow the manufacturer’s instructions. A typical removable head can be unscrewed after protecting the finish, checked for loose debris at the inlet, cleaned using only approved methods, rinsed, and reinstalled with the specified washer or seal.

Do not force parts, enlarge spray holes, or soak finishes in a chemical that the manufacturer has not approved.

EPA WaterSense-labeled showerheads use no more than 2.0 gallons per minute and must satisfy performance criteria. Lower flow than an older fixture does not necessarily mean defective pressure. [2]

Check the hose, diverter, and controls

For a handheld or combination system, inspect for a kinked hose, crushed washer, partly closed diverter, debris at a connection, or a mode selector stuck between positions.

If the shower remains weak with the head removed, the restriction is probably upstream. Do not leave the open shower arm running longer than necessary.

A pressure-balancing or thermostatic valve cartridge can also become restricted. Cartridge replacement is model-specific and may require shutting off building water.

When the whole home is affected

Possible causes include a partly closed main or meter valve, failed pressure regulator, concealed leak, frozen or damaged piping, utility maintenance, a nearby main break, or unusually high neighborhood demand.

Contact the water utility when neighbors are also affected or the change was sudden. Contact a plumber or landlord when the issue is isolated to the property.

Do not remove the flow restrictor

Removing or drilling a designed flow-control component can violate local efficiency requirements, damage the fixture, produce an unpleasant spray, increase hot-water use, conceal the actual obstruction, or void warranty coverage.

Clean and diagnose the fixture rather than converting it into an unregulated lawn sprinkler aimed at your face.

Bottom line

Compare fixtures, separate hot from cold, measure flow if useful, and clean the showerhead according to its instructions. A local restriction is common, but a sudden whole-home drop or visible leak deserves faster escalation.

Limits and cautions

  • Fixture design and local plumbing codes vary.
  • A bucket test measures flow rate, not water pressure in pounds per square inch.
