How Long Does Cooked Rice Last in the Fridge?

Cooked rice should generally be eaten or frozen within three to four days when refrigerated at 40°F or below. Cool it promptly in shallow containers because Bacillus cereus spores can survive cooking and grow when rice remains warm too long.

What to know

  • Refrigerate cooked rice within two hours, or within one hour when the surrounding temperature is above 90°F.
  • Divide large batches into shallow containers so they cool quickly.
  • Reheat leftovers to 165°F, but do not rely on reheating to repair unsafe cooling or room-temperature storage.
  • Freeze portions before the three-to-four-day refrigerator period ends.

The direct answer

Cooked rice should generally be eaten or frozen within three to four days when refrigerated at 40°F or below.

Cool and refrigerate it within two hours under ordinary conditions, or within one hour when the surrounding temperature is above 90°F.

Cooked rice is a perishable leftover. Federal guidance gives leftovers a three-to-four-day refrigerator period. [1]

Why rice needs prompt cooling

Uncooked rice can contain spores of Bacillus cereus.

Cooking can kill many active bacterial cells without eliminating every spore. When cooked rice remains warm for too long, surviving spores can germinate and the bacteria can multiply.

USDA Agricultural Research Service has modeled B. cereus growth during cooling of cooked rice, confirming that cooling time and temperature matter. [2]

Some strains can produce toxins. Ordinary reheating may not reliably eliminate every toxin formed during unsafe storage. The solution is prompt cooling and refrigeration, not heroic reheating after the rice spent the night on the counter.

Refrigerate rice promptly

Do not leave a full pot or switched-off rice cooker at room temperature for several hours.

Instead:

  1. Remove the portion being served.
  2. Divide the remaining rice into shallow containers.
  3. Spread a large quantity into smaller portions.
  4. Refrigerate promptly.
  5. Label the cooking date.
  6. Keep the refrigerator at 40°F or below.

You do not need to wait until rice reaches room temperature before refrigerating it. Small shallow portions cool more effectively than one deep container.

How to count the days

For rice cooked Monday evening:

  • Tuesday is Day 1.
  • Wednesday is Day 2.
  • Thursday is Day 3.
  • Friday is Day 4.

Use or freeze it by the end of that period when it remained continuously refrigerated.

When the cooking date is unknown, discard it.

Store it correctly

Use a clean food-safe container with a secure lid. Keep rice away from raw meat, poultry, seafood, and their juices.

Do not repeatedly return the same serving container to the refrigerator after it has remained on the table during several meals.

Can you freeze cooked rice?

Yes.

  1. Divide it into portions.
  2. Use freezer-safe containers or bags.
  3. Remove excess air.
  4. Label the date.
  5. Freeze promptly.

Texture can become drier or more fragile after freezing. A small amount of water during reheating can restore moisture.

Freezing stops bacterial growth while the rice remains frozen. It does not correct unsafe storage before freezing.

Reheat to 165°F

Federal guidance recommends reheating leftovers to 165°F. [1]

For microwave reheating:

  • Cover the rice.
  • Add water when needed.
  • Stir or rotate.
  • Break up dense clumps.
  • Check several locations.
  • Allow the recommended standing time.

A steaming surface does not prove the center of a compact portion reached the target temperature.

Avoid repeated warming and cooling

Each cycle creates another opportunity for long room-temperature exposure, uneven reheating, and contamination.

Reheat only the portion you intend to eat. The original three-to-four-day refrigerator clock does not restart after reheating.

Rice held warm in a cooker

A rice cooker may have a keep-warm function designed to maintain a safe hot-holding temperature.

Use it only according to the exact appliance instructions. Do not assume that every cooker maintains the same temperature or that a switched-off or unplugged cooker remains safe.

When the holding temperature and duration are uncertain, discard the rice.

Smell and appearance are not enough

Discard rice when it exceeded the storage period, remained warm too long, has an unknown history, shows mold or slime, has an abnormal odor, was exposed to raw food, or the refrigerator lost safe temperature control.

Rice containing hazardous levels of bacteria or toxin may not look or smell unusual. Do not taste it to decide.

Bottom line

Refrigerate cooked rice promptly in shallow containers and use or freeze it within three to four days.

Rice left at unsafe temperatures can support Bacillus cereus growth and toxin production. Reheating is not a repair for improper cooling or overnight countertop storage.

Limits and cautions

  • The three-to-four-day period assumes continuous refrigeration at 40°F or below.
  • A rice cooker’s keep-warm safety depends on the exact appliance maintaining the required holding temperature.
  • Smell and appearance cannot prove that improperly cooled rice is safe.
