Repair or Replace an Appliance?

Repair when the appliance is safe, supported, correctly diagnosed, and the repair restores useful service at a reasonable total cost. Replace when safety, pressure-vessel or sealed-system failure, repeated faults, unavailable parts, obsolete support, installation opportunity, or total economics justify it.

Quick answer: Repair when the appliance is safe, supported, correctly diagnosed, and the repair restores useful service at a reasonable total cost. Replace when safety, pressure-vessel or sealed-system failure, repeated faults, unavailable parts, obsolete support, installation opportunity, or total economics justify it.

Safety comes first

Stop use for:

  • Smoke
  • fire
  • gas odor
  • CO alarm
  • electrical arcing
  • melted plug
  • water on electricity
  • pressure-vessel leak
  • uncontrolled hot-water discharge
  • damaged microwave door
  • drum or door failure
  • refrigerant-line damage

Do not compare repair prices while the product remains an active hazard.

Check recalls

Search CPSC by:

  • Product type
  • manufacturer
  • model
  • date code
  • serial range

CPSC states that recalled products may not lawfully be sold unless the specified remedy has been completed.[1]

A recall may provide:

  • Repair
  • replacement
  • refund
  • disposal
  • new instructions

Do not pay for a repair the recall covers.

Check warranty

Review:

  • Manufacturer warranty
  • parts warranty
  • sealed-system warranty
  • labor
  • retailer return
  • service contract
  • credit-card benefit

Report a defect during the warranty period in writing and retain records.

FTC notes that a company may have the right to repair before refunding.[2]

Obtain a diagnosis

A useful diagnosis should identify:

  • Failed component
  • Reasonable cause
  • Related damage
  • Repair procedure
  • Part availability
  • labor
  • warranty
  • risk of additional failure

Do not authorize a costly “maybe” repair without understanding the uncertainty.

A diagnostic fee may be worth paying for a complex appliance.

Repair estimate

Include:

  • Part
  • labor
  • service call
  • shipping
  • disposal
  • tax
  • travel
  • required maintenance
  • additional damage
  • permit
  • access work

Ask whether the estimate is:

  • Fixed
  • Range
  • Diagnostic only
  • Conditional

Repeat failures

Consider replacement when:

  • Same fault recurs
  • Multiple major components failed
  • Repair did not resolve symptom
  • Parts are refurbished without warranty
  • Service network is poor
  • Downtime is long
  • Appliance damages the building

One unrelated minor repair does not prove the appliance is finished.

Support and parts

Check:

  • Manufacturer support
  • Parts availability
  • Firmware
  • Service manuals
  • Authorized technicians
  • Error-code support
  • Consumables
  • Product registration

A smart appliance that loses cloud support may continue basic operation or may lose major features.

Verify exact model behavior.

Operating cost

A replacement may reduce energy or water use.

Calculate:

Annual savings
=
old annual use
-
new annual use

then multiply by rate.

Do not estimate old energy use from age alone.

Use:

  • EnergyGuide
  • safe meter
  • utility data
  • model documentation

Replacement total cost

Include:

  • Purchase
  • delivery
  • installation
  • permit
  • electrical work
  • gas work
  • water line
  • venting
  • cabinet modification
  • disposal
  • lost food
  • laundry
  • temporary equipment

A cheap replacement that requires a panel upgrade is not cheap.

Repair-versus-replace calculation

Replacement net cost
=
purchase
+
installation
+
disposal
-
verified future operating savings

Compare with:

Repair cost
+
expected near-term additional work

Do not assign fake probabilities.

Use:

  • Best case
  • expected case
  • worst case

No universal 50% rule

The common rule to replace when repair costs 50% of replacement is incomplete.

It ignores:

  • safety
  • support
  • installation
  • condition
  • downtime
  • energy
  • warranty
  • household need
  • future failures

Use a structured decision.

Decision table

FindingStronger direction
Active recall with remedyUse recall
Unsafe pressure vessel or gas problemReplace or professionally repair as required
Minor supported partRepair
Repeated major failuresReplace may be stronger
Parts unavailableReplace
Expensive installation for replacementRepair may remain attractive
Old but safe and reliableNo automatic replacement
Energy savings smallDo not justify replacement on energy alone
Household needs changedReplacement may provide functional value

Environmental and disposal

Repair can extend product life.

Replacement may reduce operating energy.

Use responsible:

  • Refrigerant recovery
  • electronics recycling
  • metal recycling
  • utility program
  • retailer haul-away

Do not abandon appliances or release refrigerant.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth repairing a 10-year-old appliance?

Age alone is not enough. Use safety, diagnosis, support, cost, and need.

Should I repair a compressor?

Compare exact sealed-system warranty, diagnosis, labor, refrigerant, and replacement cost.

Does ENERGY STAR justify replacement?

Only when verified energy savings and total ownership cost support it.

Should I repair before checking recall?

No. Check recall and warranty first.

Can a technician guarantee no other failures?

Usually not. Ask about repair warranty and related component condition.
