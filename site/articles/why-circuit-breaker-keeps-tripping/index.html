Why Does My Circuit Breaker Keep Tripping?

Turn off or unplug accessible loads and attempt one reset only when the panel and surrounding area are dry and there is no smoke, sparking, burning odor, heat, water, or shock. If the breaker trips again, leave it off and obtain qualified help.

What to know

  • A repeatedly tripping breaker is reporting a condition that needs diagnosis.
  • Disconnect accessible loads before one controlled reset.
  • Do not remove the panel cover or install a larger breaker.
  • A second trip, immediate retrip, heat, smoke, or water requires qualified assistance.

A circuit breaker that repeatedly trips is reporting a condition that needs to be identified.

Possible causes include an overloaded circuit, a short circuit, a ground fault, abnormal electrical arcing, a defective appliance, damaged wiring, or a failing breaker. Do not repeatedly reset it or replace it with a higher-rated breaker.

What to know

  • Stop immediately for smoke, sparking, burning odor, heat, water, or shock.
  • Turn off or unplug accessible loads before attempting one reset.
  • A tripped handle can rest between the On and Off positions.
  • Move it fully to Off before returning it to On.
  • If it trips again, leave it off and obtain qualified help.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises households to treat frequent breaker trips, warm or discolored electrical equipment, damaged cords, and other abnormal electrical conditions as safety warnings rather than inconveniences to bypass. [1]

Do not approach a dangerous panel

Do not touch or operate the panel when standing water is present, the enclosure is wet, smoke or flames are visible, arcing or loud buzzing is occurring, the cover is hot, the room smells strongly of burning insulation, or touching equipment caused a shock.

Leave the area and contact emergency services, the utility, building management, or a licensed electrician as appropriate.

Do not remove the panel cover. Household troubleshooting ends at the accessible breaker handle.

Identify what lost power

Note the affected rooms and outlets, which lights or appliances were operating, whether a heater, air conditioner, microwave, hair dryer, vacuum, or other high-load appliance had just started, whether rain or moisture was involved, and whether the breaker trips immediately or only after several minutes.

The pattern provides useful evidence.

Trips after several devices run

This pattern can suggest that the combined load is greater than the circuit is intended to carry.

Trips immediately when one device starts

The appliance, cord, plug, receptacle, or circuit may have a fault.

Trips with everything disconnected

Leave the breaker off. This points away from an ordinary combination of plug-in loads and requires qualified diagnosis.

Trips during wet conditions

A ground fault or water intrusion may be involved. Do not continue experimenting around damp outlets, exterior wiring, bathrooms, kitchens, basements, or flooded areas.

Perform one controlled reset

Attempt a single reset only when no danger signs are present.

  1. Turn off switches and unplug accessible devices on the affected circuit.
  2. Stand on a dry floor with dry hands.
  3. Leave the panel cover in place.
  4. Move the breaker handle firmly to the full Off position.
  5. Move it to On.
  6. Observe without reconnecting loads immediately.

If the breaker trips immediately, leave it off.

Reconnect loads one at a time

When the reset holds, reconnect one device, operate it normally, wait long enough to observe, and continue one device at a time.

Stop when the breaker trips or another warning sign appears.

A trip associated with one appliance does not prove the appliance alone is defective. It may push an already heavily loaded circuit beyond its limit or expose a receptacle or wiring fault.

Do not move a high-load appliance onto an extension cord or power strip as a workaround.

Never install a larger breaker as a fix

The breaker rating is coordinated with the circuit wiring and installation.

A larger breaker can allow wiring to carry more current than intended. Upsizing is not a repair and can increase overheating and fire risk.

Also do not tape the handle in place, hold it On, bypass a GFCI or AFCI, replace a fuse with a larger one, or use adapters to evade the problem.

When an appliance may be responsible

Stop using an appliance with a damaged cord, scorched plug, water exposure, unusual heat, smoke, burning odor, sparks, a shock or tingling sensation, or repeated trips on suitable circuits.

Do not continue testing a suspect appliance in additional outlets.

When to call an electrician

Arrange qualified inspection when the breaker trips more than once, trips with accessible loads disconnected, the panel or breaker is hot, lights dim or brighten unpredictably, multiple circuits are affected, outlets are loose or discolored, water or rodent damage is possible, or the handle will not reset mechanically.

Renters should document the affected circuit, time, rooms, connected devices, and visible warning signs, then report the condition through the building’s emergency or maintenance process.

Bottom line

Disconnect loads and reset once only when the panel is dry and no danger signs exist. If the breaker trips again, leave it off and obtain qualified diagnosis. The breaker is not being stubborn. It is the component refusing to participate in whatever unsafe arrangement the rest of the circuit has proposed.

Limits and cautions

  • A household observation cannot distinguish every overload, short circuit, ground fault, arc fault, wiring defect, or breaker failure.
  • Electrical codes, panel designs, and landlord responsibilities vary by location and building.
