New vs. Used vs. Refurbished vs. Open-Box Appliances

Obtain the exact condition in writing. Verify model, serial, age, warranty, return rights, missing parts, installation requirements, recall status, and whether any recall remedy was completed. Inspect before accepting delivery.

Words such as:

  • Open-box
  • Refurbished
  • Reconditioned
  • Floor model
  • Used
  • Like new

do not have one universal condition standard.

Quick answer: Obtain the exact condition in writing. Verify model, serial, age, warranty, return rights, missing parts, installation requirements, recall status, and whether any recall remedy was completed. Inspect before accepting delivery.

New

A new appliance should generally be:

  • Unused
  • Complete
  • Covered by stated warranty
  • Sold through authorized channels
  • Current or clearly identified old stock

Still verify:

  • Box damage
  • manufacturing date
  • model
  • warranty start
  • return policy
  • recall
  • missing accessories

“New old stock” may have aging seals, batteries, hoses, firmware, or discontinued support.

Open-box

Open-box may mean:

  • Customer return
  • Display model
  • Damaged packaging
  • Delivery refusal
  • Unused inspection
  • Minor cosmetic damage
  • Missing accessory
  • Reboxed product

Ask:

  • Was it installed?
  • Was it powered?
  • Was water or gas connected?
  • Why was it returned?
  • Is the warranty full?
  • Is damage documented?
  • Are all parts included?

Floor model

A display may have:

  • Door wear
  • missing shelves
  • scratches
  • long light or display runtime
  • disconnected parts
  • repeated handling
  • no packaging

It may never have run under load.

A floor refrigerator that was never cooled differs from a used appliance but still needs inspection.

Refurbished

Refurbished can mean:

  • Manufacturer inspected
  • Seller repaired
  • Returned and cleaned
  • Parts replaced
  • Software reset
  • Cosmetic repair
  • Minimal testing

Ask who refurbished it and what standard was used.

Manufacturer-refurbished is not automatically perfect.

Seller-refurbished is not automatically poor.

The documentation matters.

Used

Used appliances may have unknown:

  • Age
  • maintenance
  • environment
  • repair history
  • transport history
  • pests
  • smoke exposure
  • water damage
  • overloading
  • power quality

Inspect thoroughly.

Do not buy a gas, refrigerant, pressure-vessel, or high-power appliance solely from photographs.

Recall status

CPSC advises searching recalls before buying or reselling used products.[1]

Selling a recalled product is unlawful unless the prescribed remedy has been completed.[1][2]

Verify:

  • Model
  • serial range
  • date code
  • remedy
  • proof of repair
  • permanent marking
  • current remedy availability

A seller saying “it works fine” does not satisfy a recall.

Warranty

Get in writing:

  • Manufacturer warranty
  • Seller warranty
  • Refurbishment warranty
  • Labor
  • parts
  • shipping
  • return
  • service location
  • start date

A product may be sold “as is.”

State implied-warranty rules vary.

Return policy

Check:

  • Days
  • restocking fee
  • delivery fee
  • pickup fee
  • installation
  • electrical or plumbing connection
  • cosmetic damage
  • performance defect
  • “final sale”

Test promptly within the return window.

Missing parts

Common missing items:

  • Shelves
  • door bins
  • hoses
  • power cord
  • stacking kit
  • leveling feet
  • drain hose
  • filter
  • mounting hardware
  • anti-tip bracket
  • manual

Replacement parts can be expensive or unavailable.

Cosmetic versus functional damage

Cosmetic:

  • Small side scratch
  • packaging damage
  • minor dent away from seal

Potentially functional:

  • Bent door
  • damaged gasket
  • crushed cabinet
  • kinked refrigerant line
  • bent drum
  • cracked glass
  • damaged cord
  • gas-valve impact
  • water damage
  • missing ground pin

Do not accept “cosmetic” without inspection.

Sanitation

Used:

  • refrigerator
  • washer
  • dishwasher
  • dehumidifier
  • air purifier

may contain:

  • mold
  • food
  • pests
  • grease
  • detergent
  • smoke
  • allergens

Cleaning does not repair unsafe contamination or water damage.

Replace filters and consumables as appropriate.

Delivery and transport

Transport rules differ:

  • Refrigerator orientation
  • washer shipping bolts
  • dryer drum
  • gas connections
  • water heater
  • heat pump compressor

Use manufacturer transport guidance.

A refrigerator laid down may require a specified upright waiting period.

Do not invent one universal wait time.

Frequently asked questions

Is manufacturer-refurbished safer?

It may provide clearer process and warranty, but verify exact terms.

Is open-box unused?

Not necessarily.

Can a recalled product be sold after repair?

CPSC says it may be sold when the specified remedy has been completed. Obtain proof.

Should I buy a used water heater?

Pressure-vessel age, fuel, installation, warranty, and transport make it high risk. Professional review is essential.

Does cosmetic damage matter?

It can when it affects doors, seals, insulation, wiring, tubing, or structure.
