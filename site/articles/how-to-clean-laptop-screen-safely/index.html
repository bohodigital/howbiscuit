How to Clean a Laptop Screen Without Damaging It

Shut the laptop down, disconnect power, start with a clean dry microfiber cloth, and put any manufacturer-approved liquid on the cloth rather than spraying the screen. Use light pressure and let the display dry before powering on.

Updated Aug 3, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Check the exact display instructions because matte, glossy, touchscreen, and specialty finishes can require different methods.
  • Use a clean soft microfiber or lint-free cloth rather than paper products or abrasive fabric.
  • Never spray liquid directly onto the panel or let moisture collect along the bezel and hinges.
  • Stop rubbing when a mark is actually a scratch, coating defect, pressure mark, or internal display fault.

The direct answer

For an ordinary laptop display:

  1. Shut the laptop down.
  2. Unplug its charger and accessories.
  3. Let the display cool.
  4. Wipe loose dust with a clean, dry microfiber or other soft lint-free cloth.
  5. For persistent smudges, lightly dampen the cloth using the liquid permitted by the manufacturer.
  6. Wipe gently without pressing the panel.
  7. Let the screen dry before reconnecting power.

Apple tells users to shut down and disconnect a Mac laptop, then clean its display with a soft lint-free cloth dampened with water. Dell likewise recommends a soft microfiber cloth and warns against direct spray and household cleaners. [1] [2]

Check the exact display instructions

Laptop screens can have glossy coatings, matte antiglare surfaces, touchscreen layers, oleophobic coatings, privacy filters, or specialty textures.

The safest liquid and cloth can differ. Use the manufacturer’s current cleaning page or manual.

Do not assume a cleaning method approved for a glass phone screen is approved for a matte laptop panel.

Turn the laptop off first

Shutting down makes residue easier to see, lets the panel and chassis cool, prevents accidental input, and reduces the risk of moisture reaching energized circuitry.

Disconnect power, docks, external monitors, USB devices, headphones, and wired networking.

Do not clean a display that is cracked, unusually hot, swollen, or separating from the chassis.

Use the right cloth

Use a clean microfiber cloth, soft lint-free display cloth, or manufacturer-supplied polishing cloth.

Avoid paper towels, facial tissue, toilet paper, rough towels, dirty shop rags, abrasive sponges, and clothing containing grit.

Particles trapped in a reused cloth can scratch or abrade a coating.

Start dry

A dry cloth is often sufficient for dust, light fingerprints, and loose particles.

Support the back of the display gently if needed without twisting the hinges. Use light overlapping passes rather than pressing harder on one stubborn spot.

LCD panels can show pressure marks or suffer internal damage even when the outer surface remains intact.

Dampen the cloth, not the screen

Never spray liquid directly onto the display.

Apple and Dell warn that liquid can run beneath the bezel or into openings and damage electronics. [1] [2]

The cloth should be slightly damp, not saturated or dripping. Keep moisture away from bezel seams, webcam and microphone openings, hinges, keyboard, ports, and speaker grilles.

Which liquid should you use?

Follow the exact manufacturer instructions.

Water or distilled water is a common starting choice. Some manufacturers permit specific screen cleaners or limited alcohol use on particular surfaces, while others warn against alcohol and ordinary chemical cleaners.

Dell’s monitor-cleaning guidance recommends water or a solution designed for the display and warns against detergents, ammonia, solvents, abrasive cleaner, alcohol, and compressed air on covered monitor products. [2]

Apple permits specific methods for its own products but still requires model-appropriate care. [1]

Avoid household glass cleaner

Do not use window cleaner, ammonia, acetone, bleach, furniture polish, scouring products, or aerosol disinfectant unless the exact display manufacturer explicitly permits the substance.

A laptop screen is not a kitchen window, despite both objects occasionally displaying an unpleasant reflection.

Wipe gently

Use overlapping light passes. Do not scrape dried residue with a fingernail, use a razor, rub one pixel-sized location aggressively, press the display into the keyboard, or hold the laptop by the panel.

For hardened food, adhesive, paint, or unknown chemical residue, contact the manufacturer or a qualified repair provider before experimenting.

Is the mark actually dirt?

A spot that does not change after safe cleaning may be a scratch, coating wear, pressure damage, dead pixel, delamination, keyboard contact mark, or internal panel defect.

Do not continue rubbing harder. A plain-color display test can help separate surface contamination from a panel fault.

Touchscreens

Clean touchscreens with the same restraint. Remove rings and watches that could strike the panel, keep fluid away from the digitizer edge, and confirm touch input only after the display is fully dry.

Bottom line

Power the laptop down, disconnect it, and begin with a clean dry microfiber cloth.

When moisture is necessary, lightly dampen the cloth using only a method approved for the exact display. Never spray the screen directly, use window cleaner casually, or press harder because one mark refuses to cooperate.

Limits and cautions

  • Cleaning liquids approved by one manufacturer may be prohibited by another.
  • This guide does not cover chemical, paint, adhesive, or liquid damage inside a display.
  • Specialty nano-texture and professionally calibrated displays can require dedicated cloths and procedures.
