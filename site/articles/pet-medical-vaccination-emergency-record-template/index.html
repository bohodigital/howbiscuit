Pet Medical, Vaccination, Medication, and Emergency Record Template

A pet record should be usable during routine care, boarding, travel, evacuation, poisoning, and after-hours emergencies even when the usual veterinary clinic is closed.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • A pet record should be usable during routine care, boarding, travel, evacuation, poisoning, and after-hours emergencies even when the usual veterinary clinic is closed.
  • Keep identification, contacts, diagnoses, medicines, vaccinations, test results, proof of ownership, emergency authorization, and evacuation information together.

Create one record for each animal.

Keep:

  • Protected digital copy
  • Printed emergency copy
  • Copy in the evacuation kit
  • Current version with the pet sitter or caregiver where appropriate

A preparedness file should include veterinary records, rabies and vaccination certificates, medical summaries, prescriptions, recent test results, proof of ownership, descriptions, and photographs.

  • Stop date

Prescription drugs should be given only under veterinary authorization and stored under the manufacturer’s conditions.

Requirements depend on species, age, health, location, boarding, travel, and exposure risk.

Preserve the official rabies certificate.

Laboratory and preventive-care record

Track:

  • Heartworm test
  • FeLV/FIV test
  • Fecal test
  • Bloodwork
  • Urinalysis
  • Imaging
  • Dental care
  • Parasite prevention
  • Weight trend

Recent heartworm results for dogs and FeLV/FIV results for cats can be useful in emergency and boarding records.

Have formal authorization reviewed for local legal and clinic requirements.

Do not leave full card numbers or passwords in the ordinary printed record.

First-aid record

Keep:

  • Medical summary
  • Medication list
  • Veterinarian
  • Emergency clinic
  • Poison contacts
  • Muzzle or restraint limitations
  • Safe transport method

Bring a copy of the pet’s medical record and medication information during an emergency.

Do not give human medication unless a veterinarian specifically directs it.

Travel and evacuation

Include:

  • Carrier location
  • Ownership proof
  • Vaccination records
  • Health certificate
  • Destination rules
  • Pet-friendly lodging
  • Food
  • Water
  • Medication
  • Recent photograph
  • Caregiver contact

Check current vaccination and travel-document requirements before every trip.

Obtain records from the clinic

Ask for:

  • Medical summary
  • Vaccination certificates
  • Laboratory results
  • Medication instructions
  • Imaging reports
  • Discharge instructions

The veterinary practice generally retains the original record under applicable law, while owners should know how to obtain needed copies.

Bottom line

Keep identification, contacts, diagnoses, medicines, vaccinations, test results, proof of ownership, emergency authorization, and evacuation information together.

An emergency is already a poor time for clear thought. It is a particularly poor time to explain a medication by saying it is “the small white tablet from the bottle that might be in the kitchen.”

Quick summary

Keep one current record with identification, veterinarian contacts, diagnoses, allergies, vaccinations, medications, tests, microchip data, insurance, emergency consent, and evacuation documents.

A pet record should be usable during routine care, boarding, travel, evacuation, poisoning, and after-hours emergencies even when the usual veterinary clinic is closed.
