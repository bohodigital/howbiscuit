Home Paint Color, Product, Sheen, and Touch-Up Record Template

A color name alone is rarely enough to reproduce a finish. Touch-ups depend on the exact product, sheen, formula, substrate, age, lighting, batch, and application method.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Testing not applicable
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • A color name alone is rarely enough to reproduce a finish. Touch-ups depend on the exact product, sheen, formula, substrate, age, lighting, batch, and application method.
  • Record the exact color, formula, base, sheen, product, surface, date, and application method.

Create one entry for every painted surface.

Record more than the color name.

A useful paint record includes:

  • Manufacturer
  • Product line
  • Color name
  • Color number
  • Tint formula
  • Base
  • Sheen
  • Interior or exterior use
  • Primer
  • Application method
  • Date
  • Lot or batch
  • Leftover-paint location

Sherwin-Williams advises matching color, sheen, texture, and application method when performing touch-ups.

Use exact surface names:

  • Living room north wall
  • Kitchen ceiling
  • Primary bedroom trim
  • Front door exterior
  • Bathroom vanity
  • Basement floor

Do not record only gray walls.

Future-you will discover that the building contains six grays, three off-whites, and one ceiling painted with whatever remained in a bucket.

Photograph every label

Capture:

  • Front label
  • Formula sticker
  • Barcode
  • Product number
  • Batch or lot
  • Color code
  • Purchase receipt
  • Safety warnings
  • Date opened

Store the images with the room record.

A color photograph of the wall is useful context but is not a reliable formula record because camera processing and lighting change color appearance.

Record sheen precisely

Common sheen names include:

  • Flat
  • Matte
  • Eggshell
  • Satin
  • Semigloss
  • Gloss

Manufacturers do not always define these categories identically.

Sheen changes:

  • Light reflection
  • Perceived color
  • Washability
  • Touch-up visibility
  • Surface-defect visibility

Sherwin-Williams notes that sheen can alter perceived color and that matching the original finish improves touch-up consistency.

Record the application method

Touch-up appearance can differ when the original coat was:

  • Rolled
  • Brushed
  • Sprayed
  • Padded

Record:

  • Roller nap
  • Brush type
  • Spray system
  • Number of coats
  • Direction
  • Texture

Sherwin-Williams recommends using the original application method where possible because a brushed repair can look different from a rolled wall even with paint from the same can.

Store paint according to the manufacturer’s temperature and container instructions.

Do not:

  • Leave where children can reach it
  • Store beside food
  • Transfer to an unlabeled drink container
  • Keep a rusting leaking can indefinitely
  • Pour unwanted paint into a drain

Check the local household hazardous-waste or paint-recycling program.

Touch-ups can remain visible because of:

  • Aging
  • Sunlight
  • Dirt
  • Different batch
  • Different sheen
  • Different tool
  • Different wall texture
  • Temperature and humidity

Sherwin-Williams notes that flat and matte finishes are often easier to touch up than satin or semigloss finishes.

Matching an unknown paint

When the original record is missing:

  1. Check old cans.
  2. Contact the painter or builder.
  3. Review receipts.
  4. Search property records.
  5. Use a clean physical sample where safe.
  6. Ask a paint store to scan the sample.
  7. Test the match in a hidden area.
  8. Let it dry completely before judging.

Sherwin-Williams describes controlled-light scanning as more reliable than matching from an ordinary phone photograph alone.

Do not cut a wall sample from a pre-1978 surface without resolving the lead-safety question first.

Lead-safe record

For pre-1978 components, record:

  • Inspection or risk assessment
  • Lead test
  • Certified renovator
  • Work area
  • Date
  • Containment
  • Cleaning verification
  • Contractor certification
  • Final report

EPA says contractors disturbing covered surfaces in pre-1978 homes generally must follow its Renovation, Repair and Painting requirements unless an applicable exemption or documented lead-free determination applies.

Bottom line

Record the exact color, formula, base, sheen, product, surface, date, and application method.

A paint name is a useful beginning. It is not enough to recreate a finish years later after the formula, product line, sheen, lighting, and remaining half-can have all wandered into history.
