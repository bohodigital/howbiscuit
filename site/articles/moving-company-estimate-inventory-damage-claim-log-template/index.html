Moving Company Estimate, Inventory, Delivery, and Damage Claim Log Template

Interstate movers must provide specified documents and written estimates, but state rules control many local moves. A complete file makes it harder for verbal promises, undocumented damage, and surprise charges to replace the actual agreement.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 4 min read Researched Testing not applicable

What to know

  • Interstate movers must provide specified documents and written estimates, but state rules control many local moves. A complete file makes it harder for verbal promises, undocumented damage, and surprise charges to replace the actual agreement.
  • Preserve the estimate, survey, liability selection, inventory, bill of lading, delivery notes, photographs, and claim deadline.

Create one project file before requesting quotes.

For an interstate move, verify the mover or broker through FMCSA and preserve:

  • Written estimate
  • Survey
  • Rights-and-responsibilities booklet
  • Order for service
  • Inventory
  • Bill of lading
  • Liability selection
  • Receipts
  • Delivery records
  • Claim correspondence

FMCSA requires interstate movers to provide written estimates and ordinarily base them on an in-person or virtual survey unless the customer waives that survey in writing.

FMCSA says a verbal quote is not an official estimate and that the written estimate must include transportation, accessorial, and advanced charges.

Do not compare totals until every quote covers the same:

  • Inventory
  • Packing
  • Access
  • Dates
  • Storage
  • Liability level

Inventory before pickup

Create your own inventory with:

  • Item ID
  • Description
  • Room
  • Condition
  • Photograph
  • Serial number
  • Approximate value
  • Weight where relevant
  • Box number
  • Extraordinary-value status

Then compare it with the mover’s inventory.

FMCSA requires interstate movers to prepare an inventory before or during loading, describe existing damage or unusual wear, and obtain signatures on each page. Customers can note disagreements before signing.

Do not sign a blank or unreadable inventory.

Valuable items

FMCSA describes items worth more than one hundred dollars per pound as articles of extraordinary value for some liability purposes.

List them explicitly when required.

Examples may include:

  • Jewelry
  • Art
  • China
  • Collectibles
  • Small expensive electronics

Consider transporting irreplaceable records, medications, and high-value personal items separately.

Liability choice

Full Value Protection

The mover is generally responsible for repair, replacement with a similar item, or a cash settlement within the selected coverage terms.

Released Value Protection

Federal interstate rules limit ordinary released-value liability to no more than sixty cents per pound per article.

A 25-pound television would therefore have maximum released-value liability of fifteen dollars at sixty cents per pound.

FMCSA explains both options and says Released Value requires a specific signed selection.

Do not call Released Value “insurance.” It is a limited carrier-liability option.

Do not allow possessions to leave without a copy of the bill of lading and inventory.

Record every change in writing.

Delivery inspection

Before the crew leaves:

  1. Match item and box numbers.
  2. Note missing items.
  3. Photograph exterior damage.
  4. Open visibly damaged boxes where practical.
  5. Inspect large furniture and appliances.
  6. Record wall and floor damage.
  7. Note disagreement on the delivery documents.
  8. Preserve packaging.
  9. Obtain the driver’s acknowledgment.

Do not sign a statement that everything arrived in good condition when inspection has not occurred.

Complaint versus claim

A damage claim seeks compensation from the mover.

An FMCSA complaint reports possible regulatory violations or fraud.

FMCSA says it does not resolve private claims but uses complaints to identify companies that may warrant investigation.

Do both when appropriate, but do not confuse one with the other.

Bottom line

Preserve the estimate, survey, liability selection, inventory, bill of lading, delivery notes, photographs, and claim deadline.

A move already involves putting nearly every physical possession into the custody of strangers. The paperwork should not also be placed into the custody of memory and goodwill.
