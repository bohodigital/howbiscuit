Home Improvement Permit and Inspection Log Template

Permit rules are local and project-specific. A complete log preserves which work was approved, which inspections occurred before concealment, and whether the project received final closure.

Updated Aug 5, 2026 3 min read Researched Not hands-on tested

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Permit rules are local and project-specific. A complete log preserves which work was approved, which inspections occurred before concealment, and whether the project received final closure.
  • Track the permit from application through final closure.

Contact the city, county, or other authority having jurisdiction before construction begins.

Record every permit, approved plan, revision, inspection, correction, and final approval.

Local permit processes commonly require staged inspections before structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, insulation, or other work is concealed.

Possible permits include building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, demolition, roofing, driveway, sewer, sign, historic review, and right-of-way.

Do not assume a small project is exempt.

Rules can depend on structural work, location, cost, occupancy, electrical or plumbing changes, fire protection, and historic status.

Confirm through official databases.

Do not accept a claim that permits are unnecessary merely because a contractor prefers to begin immediately.

The exact stages vary.

Do not allow drywall, concrete, soil, or finishes to conceal work before the required inspection.

Photograph corrected work before concealment.

Revisions

Changes after permit approval may require revised drawings and renewed review.

Record:

  • Change
  • Reason
  • Price
  • Schedule effect
  • Designer approval
  • Permit revision
  • Inspection effect

Do not proceed with changed work merely because everyone at the site agreed verbally.

Final approval

Collect:

  • Final inspection
  • Closed permit record
  • Certificate of occupancy where required
  • Electrical approval
  • Plumbing approval
  • Mechanical approval
  • Energy documents
  • Warranty
  • As-built drawing
  • Lien releases where applicable

Do not make final payment solely because the contractor says the inspector “basically passed it.”

Monitor permit issuance and inspection results directly through the official property record where available.

Bottom line

Track the permit from application through final closure.

The useful question is not merely whether someone pulled a permit. It is whether the approved work was inspected at the required stages, corrections were resolved, and the government record eventually stopped describing the project as open.

Quick summary

Track the project scope, permit authority, application, approved drawings, contractor, revisions, inspections, corrections, final approval, and certificate before walls close or final payment leaves.

Permit rules are local and project-specific. A complete log preserves which work was approved, which inspections occurred before concealment, and whether the project received final closure.
