Appliance Buying Checklist: Measurements, Hookups, Delivery, Warranty, and Recalls

Before buying a major appliance, verify:

  1. It fits the installation space.
  2. It fits through the delivery route.
  3. The building has the required hookups.
  4. Ventilation and clearances are adequate.
  5. Installation is permitted and safe.
  6. Warranty and return terms are acceptable.
  7. The exact model is not recalled.
  8. Delivery condition is documented.

Quick answer: Measure the opening, appliance, doors, hallways, stairs, elevator, and door swing. Identify voltage, circuit, gas, water, drain, vent, condensate, and floor requirements. Inspect before signing.

Measure the installation space

Record:

  • Width
  • height
  • depth
  • baseboard
  • counter overhang
  • trim
  • doors
  • hinges
  • utility protrusions
  • floor slope

Measure in several places.

Walls and floors are not always square.

FTC advises measuring the installation space and allowing room for doors and safe ventilation.[1]

Measure the appliance

Use the manufacturer specification, not only retailer dimensions.

Check:

  • Cabinet dimensions
  • door
  • handle
  • hinge
  • required clearance
  • open-door depth
  • hose and cord space
  • vent
  • anti-tip bracket
  • leveling range

A refrigerator depth may exclude handles.

A dryer depth may increase with the vent.

Delivery route

Measure:

  • Exterior door
  • interior door
  • hallway
  • turn
  • stair width
  • landing
  • ceiling
  • railing
  • elevator
  • doorway trim
  • gate
  • porch
  • slope

Ask whether doors or handles can be removed.

Do not assume the delivery team will dismantle the building.

Door and lid swing

Check:

  • Refrigerator door
  • oven door
  • dishwasher door
  • washer lid
  • washer door
  • dryer door
  • filter access
  • service access

The appliance may fit closed but block the walkway when open.

Electrical requirements

Verify:

  • Voltage
  • amperage
  • dedicated circuit
  • receptacle type
  • grounding
  • cord included or separate
  • plug orientation
  • disconnect
  • GFCI or AFCI requirements
  • panel capacity

Do not use:

  • extension cord
  • adapter
  • ungrounded conversion
  • undersized circuit

Major appliances should be connected according to manufacturer instructions and code.

Gas

Verify:

  • Fuel type
  • pressure
  • shutoff
  • connector
  • venting
  • combustion air
  • permit
  • professional installation
  • CO alarms

Natural gas and propane are not interchangeable without an approved conversion.

Do not use an old connector automatically.

Water supply

Check:

  • Shutoff
  • pressure
  • pipe condition
  • connector
  • flood protection
  • filter
  • hot/cold requirement
  • water hardness
  • leak pan

Appliances may require new hoses rather than reused hoses.

Drain

Verify:

  • Standpipe
  • sink connection
  • floor drain
  • drain height
  • air gap
  • hose routing
  • pump capacity
  • code

Do not create an airtight washing-machine drain connection unless required.

Venting

Dryers, gas appliances, range hoods, and some water heaters require specific venting.

Check:

  • Material
  • diameter
  • length
  • elbows
  • termination
  • clearance
  • shared vent
  • condensate
  • code

Do not assume an existing vent is reusable.

Condensate

Possible sources:

  • Heat-pump water heater
  • high-efficiency gas appliance
  • heat-pump dryer
  • dehumidifier
  • air conditioner

Plan:

  • Gravity drain
  • pump
  • freeze protection
  • service access
  • overflow protection

Floor and structure

Check:

  • Level
  • strength
  • water resistance
  • vibration
  • pedestal
  • drain pan
  • seismic strapping where required
  • anti-tip bracket
  • clearance from combustibles

A large refrigerator or water heater can impose substantial weight.

Model identity

Record:

  • Manufacturer
  • model
  • variant
  • color
  • fuel
  • voltage
  • capacity
  • hardware version
  • certification
  • included accessories

Do not accept a substitution without reviewing all specifications again.

EnergyGuide and ENERGY STAR

Use:

  • Annual kWh or fuel
  • local rate
  • current ENERGY STAR status
  • capacity
  • feature comparison

Do not rely on the retailer’s monthly cost graphic.

Warranty and service

Read:

  • Manufacturer warranty
  • labor
  • parts
  • sealed system
  • registration
  • service area
  • authorized service
  • extended contract
  • return period

Do not buy a service contract before reading exclusions.

Recall check

Search CPSC using:

  • Manufacturer
  • model
  • date code
  • serial range

Check again before accepting used or open-box equipment.

Record the result.

Delivery inspection

Before signing:

  • Match model.
  • Inspect packaging.
  • Inspect all sides.
  • Check doors.
  • Check glass.
  • Check controls.
  • Check cord.
  • Check hoses.
  • Check accessories.
  • Photograph damage.
  • Note concealed-damage procedure.
  • Keep documents.

Do not allow pressure to rush the inspection.

Installation

Confirm:

  • Installer identity
  • permit
  • old-appliance removal
  • leveling
  • anti-tip
  • leak test
  • gas test
  • vent
  • drain
  • electrical connection
  • startup
  • temperature
  • test cycle

Do not sign completion before basic operation is verified.

First-run verification

Refrigerator

  • Temperature reaches safe range
  • doors seal
  • water line does not leak

Washer

  • Fills
  • drains
  • spins
  • no leak
  • shipping bolts removed

Dryer

  • Heats
  • tumbles
  • exterior vent opens
  • no gas odor

Dishwasher

  • Fills
  • drains
  • no leak
  • door seals

Water heater

  • No leak
  • correct vent
  • safe delivered temperature
  • relief discharge installed

Frequently asked questions

Should I measure the doorway or appliance first?

Both, including handles, packaging, turns, and open-door depth.

Does delivery include installation?

Not automatically. Read the service scope.

Can I reuse cords and hoses?

Only when manufacturer and code permit it. Many installers require new approved connections.

Should I accept a dent discount?

Only after confirming the damage is cosmetic and warranty/return terms are documented.

When should I register the appliance?

Follow the warranty instructions and keep proof of purchase.
