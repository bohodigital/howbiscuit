Why Does My Monitor Say No Signal? Test the Display Chain in Order

A no-signal message means the monitor has enough power to display its own status but is not receiving usable video. Select the correct input, reconnect both ends of the cable, use the correct graphics port, bypass docks and adapters, cross-test the monitor and computer, and then inspect resolution, refresh rate, and drivers.

What to know

  • Separate no power from no signal before replacing hardware.
  • Desktop monitors usually connect to a discrete graphics card when one is installed.
  • Bypass docks and adapters to simplify the display path.
  • Cross-testing one component at a time identifies the failed monitor, cable, port, or computer output.

A no-signal message means the monitor has power but is not receiving a compatible video signal.

Treat the setup as a chain: computer, graphics port, adapter or dock, cable, and monitor input.

Start with input selection and cable seating. Then bypass intermediate equipment and test the monitor and computer separately.

Separate no power from no signal

The monitor likely has no power when its indicator is off, its menu will not appear, and it shows no manufacturer logo with a known-working outlet and power cable.

It has power but lacks video when its menu appears, it displays “No Signal” or “No Input,” or it enters standby after searching for a source.

A monitor that displays its own menu has confirmed that some panel electronics and backlighting work. It has not proven that every input is functional.

Select the correct input

Open the monitor’s source menu and choose the port actually connected, such as HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-C, DVI, or VGA.

Disable automatic input selection temporarily when the monitor keeps choosing another source.

Dell places correct input selection and secure cable connection among the first checks for a no-display condition. [1]

Reconnect both ends

Turn the monitor and computer off. Disconnect the video cable at both ends, inspect for bent pins or damage, reconnect each end fully, tighten connector screws where present, then power the monitor and computer again.

Some DisplayPort connectors have a locking tab. Release it rather than yanking the plug out and converting a cable problem into a soldering problem.

Use the correct computer port

Desktop computers may expose video connectors in two places: motherboard ports near USB and network connectors, and a separate graphics card lower in the case.

When a discrete graphics card is installed, the monitor normally connects to that card. Some processors lack integrated graphics, so the motherboard ports remain inactive even though they physically exist.

Dell identifies connection to the wrong video output as a common cause of no display. [1]

Bypass docks and adapters

Temporarily remove USB-C or Thunderbolt docks, HDMI switches, KVM switches, USB display adapters, signal converters, AV receivers, and wall plates.

Connect the monitor directly to the computer using the simplest supported cable.

When the direct connection works, reconnect one component at a time. This identifies whether the failure belongs to the dock, adapter, cable, power supply, firmware, or port.

USB-C deserves special attention because the connector shape does not guarantee video capability. The source port, cable, dock, and monitor must support the required display mode.

Cross-test every component

Connect the monitor to another computer, connect the computer to another monitor or television, try another known-good cable, try another input on the same monitor, and try another graphics output.

Change one component per test. Altering the cable, monitor, adapter, and port together proves only that several things changed, which is technically information but not useful information.

Ask the operating system to detect the display

On Windows, open Display settings, select Detect where available, use Windows plus P to choose Duplicate or Extend, and restart with the monitor connected.

On macOS, open Displays settings, reconnect the display, and use the supported detection controls. Apple recommends verifying power and cabling, reconnecting the display, and checking detection when an external screen is not recognized. [2]

Reduce display demands

A monitor may reject a mode that exceeds the screen, cable, adapter, dock, or port.

Set the affected monitor to its recommended resolution, choose a standard refresh rate such as 60 Hz for diagnosis, disable HDR temporarily, disconnect extra monitors, and reconnect the affected display.

Restore higher refresh rates, HDR, and multi-monitor arrangements one at a time after the picture is stable.

Update official software

Use official sources for graphics drivers, dock firmware, computer BIOS or system firmware, monitor firmware where available, and operating-system updates.

Avoid generic driver-updater programs. Those excel mainly at transforming one known problem into several more mysterious ones.

When hardware is likely

Service or replacement becomes more likely when the monitor fails with several computers and cables, one input never works, the computer produces no video on any screen, the graphics adapter disappears from the operating system, a connector is loose, or the image shows persistent lines and corruption.

Stop using equipment with burning odor, arcing, severe heat, swollen components, or visibly damaged connectors.

Bottom line

Verify monitor power, choose the correct input, reconnect the cable, use the correct graphics port, bypass adapters, cross-test each component, and then inspect display settings and drivers. Most no-signal failures can be isolated without replacing everything on the desk in a burst of retail therapy.

Limits and cautions

  • Supported display modes and monitor counts depend on the computer, graphics hardware, ports, cable, and operating system.
  • A monitor that shows its menu has not proven that every video input works.
  • This article does not test a specific display chain.
