Is Food in a Dented Can Safe to Eat?

A shallow dent away from every seam may be acceptable when the can is otherwise intact. Discard cans with deep or sharp dents, dents on the top, bottom, or side seams, bulging, leaking, rust-through, spurting contents, or abnormal odor.

Updated Aug 3, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested

What to know

  • The location and depth of a dent matter more than the mere presence of a cosmetic mark.
  • Seam damage can compromise the seal even when no hole is visible.
  • Do not open, taste, puncture, or press a bulging or leaking can.
  • A printed date does not make a damaged container safe.

The direct answer

A can with one small shallow dent may be usable when it is otherwise intact, not leaking, not bulging, not rusted through, not punctured, and not damaged along a seam.

USDA says food in a can with a small dent can be safe when the container is otherwise in good condition and advises discarding deeply dented cans. [1]

When the dent is deep, sharp, or located on a seam, discard the can.

Why seams matter

A food can is sealed at the top, bottom, and, on many designs, a vertical side seam.

A dent can crack the seal, create a microscopic leak, damage the interior protective lining, permit contamination, or begin corrosion. Damage can be present even when food is not visibly leaking.

What counts as a serious dent?

Treat the dent as serious when you can place a finger deeply into it, it has a sharp crease, metal is folded, the dent crosses a seam, the can will not stand normally, a lid or bottom is distorted, the can rocks because an end changed shape, or rust is developing in the damaged area.

Do not hammer the dent flat and reconsider the container after removing the visible evidence.

Discard bulging cans

A swollen or bulging can can indicate gas production inside the container.

Do not open it, taste it, puncture it, press the lid, smell it closely, or feed it to animals.

USDA identifies bulging, leaking, badly dented, and otherwise compromised containers as warning signs that require disposal. [1]

Keep the can away from children and animals and follow local disposal or public-health instructions when botulism is suspected.

Leaking or spurting cans

Discard a can that leaks, hisses abnormally, spurts liquid or foam, releases an abnormal odor, contains discolored or foaming food, or has a loose or lifted lid.

Do not taste the contents. Botulism toxin cannot be reliably detected by appearance, smell, or taste.

Rust

Light surface rust that wipes away may be cosmetic. Discard the can when rust cannot be removed, has pitted the metal, appears around a seam, creates a pinhole, accompanies a dent or leak, or weakens the container.

When you cannot tell whether rust has penetrated, discard the can.

A can dented at home

A can dropped in the pantry can develop a shallow sidewall dent away from the seams. If it remains otherwise intact and the dent is truly minor, USDA says the food may remain usable. [1]

Use it sooner rather than storing a damaged can indefinitely.

Discard it when the dent is deep, a seam is involved, the can leaks, an end bulges, or you cannot confidently classify the damage.

Dates do not override damage

A best-by or use-by date does not make a compromised can safe.

Shelf-stable food can remain usable for long periods when the container remains sound. The package is the preservation system. Once its integrity is uncertain, the printed date has little to contribute.

After opening an acceptable can

Inspect without leaning over the container.

Discard the food when it has abnormal odor, foam or gas, mold, unusual color, unexpected texture, damaged internal lining, or other evidence of contamination.

Transfer unused food to a clean covered refrigerator container.

Possible foodborne illness

Seek urgent medical care for difficulty swallowing, double or blurred vision, drooping eyelids, muscle weakness, slurred speech, or difficulty breathing. Possible botulism is a medical emergency.

Bottom line

A small shallow dent away from every seam may be acceptable when the can is otherwise intact.

Discard deeply dented cans, seam dents, bulging cans, leaks, rust-through, spurting contents, and containers with abnormal odor or appearance.

Limits and cautions

  • Visual inspection cannot prove that a damaged seam remains hermetic.
  • This guide applies to ordinary commercially canned food, not home-canning failures or emergency-contaminated products.
  • Suspected botulism containers may require local public-health disposal guidance.
