How to Check a Used Dishwasher Before You Buy It

Verify the exact model, recall status, dimensions, plumbing, and electrical requirements, then run a complete fill, wash, drain, and dry cycle. Reject a machine that leaks, leaves dirty standing water, produces electrical odor, or cannot complete the cycle.

Updated Aug 3, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Record the model and serial number and search the CPSC recall database.
  • Inspect the door seal, racks, spray arms, filter, tub, hoses, cord, and junction area before connecting power.
  • A useful test must include filling, spraying, drainage, heating or drying, and a leak check.
  • Include missing racks, installation hardware, hoses, delivery, and immediate repairs in the real purchase cost.

The direct answer

Do not buy a used dishwasher until you have verified:

  1. Manufacturer, model, serial number, and age.
  2. Recall status.
  3. Cabinet dimensions and door clearance.
  4. Electrical connection.
  5. Water-supply and drain requirements.
  6. Door alignment and gasket condition.
  7. Racks, spray arms, filter, and tub.
  8. Filling, washing, draining, and drying.
  9. Absence of leaks and error codes.
  10. Delivery, installation, repair, and operating requirements.
  11. A written receipt and return terms.

A dishwasher that powers on has completed only the easiest part of the inspection.

Record the exact identity

Photograph the manufacturer, model, serial number, electrical rating, manufacturing date when available, EnergyGuide label, and included parts.

Similar product names can hide different pumps, racks, heaters, controls, and recall coverage.

Search for recalls

Search the CPSC database using the exact brand, model, serial range, and production date. [1]

Obtain proof that any official remedy was completed. Do not accept “it has always worked” as a substitute for recall compliance.

Measure the opening

Record cabinet width, opening height, depth, countertop overhang, flooring height, door swing, distance to sink plumbing, water valve, drain connection, and electrical location.

A dishwasher can fit between cabinets while failing to clear the countertop, floor, door, or plumbing.

Use the exact installation manual.

Inspect the door and gasket

Look for:

  • Torn or hardened gasket.
  • Missing corner baffles.
  • Bent hinge.
  • Loose latch.
  • Door sag.
  • Cracked inner panel.
  • Rust.
  • Signs of previous leakage.

The door should close squarely without lifting or force.

Inspect the tub and racks

Reject or price carefully when you find a cracked tub, severe rust, damaged heating element, melted plastic, broken rack wheels, exposed rack metal, missing filter, cracked spray arm, heavy scale, sewage odor, or pest evidence.

Replacement racks and baskets can be unexpectedly expensive, because molded wire apparently becomes a precious material when attached to an appliance model number.

Rotate the spray arms

With power disconnected, rotate each spray arm gently where the design permits.

Check for binding, cracks, blocked jets, missing fasteners, and whether rack contents will strike the arm.

Do not enlarge spray holes with drill bits or sharp metal tools.

Inspect the filter and sump

Remove the filter only through the exact manual.

Look for broken mesh, glass, food, paper labels, grease, rust, missing covers, heavy odor, and dirty standing water.

Whirlpool says filters and drain paths can collect debris that interferes with drainage and cleaning. [3]

A small amount of clean water in a protected filter area can be normal on some designs. Dirty water covering the tub is not the same condition.

Run a complete test cycle

The strongest test is a full normal cycle in a stable installed location.

Confirm that the dishwasher:

  • Fills.
  • Begins spraying.
  • Changes sound as spray arms rotate.
  • Does not grind.
  • Opens the detergent dispenser.
  • Drains.
  • Refills when expected.
  • Completes without an error.
  • Heats or dries according to the selected cycle.
  • Does not leak.

Place dry paper towels around the front and accessible hose connections to reveal small leaks.

Do not test a loose built-in machine using improvised water, drain, or electrical connections.

Check drainage

Inspect the drain hose for kinks, cracks, improper routing, a clogged air gap, disposal connection, or evidence of dirty-water backflow.

Whirlpool identifies filters, drain hoses, air gaps, and disposal connections as common drainage checkpoints. [3]

A new disposal can block dishwasher drainage when its inlet was never opened during installation.

Inspect hoses and wiring

Reject burned cords, exposed wiring, improvised splices, melted junction components, brittle supply hoses, leaking inlet valves, damaged drain hoses, and water staining around electrical parts.

Built-in installation can require a dedicated circuit, grounding, an approved water connection, and local code compliance.

Compare energy and water use

ENERGY STAR advises comparing annual energy use, water consumption, capacity, and cycle features among dishwashers. [2]

Use the exact model’s EnergyGuide information rather than assuming age alone determines operating cost.

Bottom line

Verify recalls, dimensions, and installation requirements before testing performance.

Then run a full cycle and check filling, spraying, drainage, heat, controls, noise, and every accessible leak point. A dishwasher that cannot be tested should be treated as an unverified repair project.

Limits and cautions

  • A small amount of clean water in some filter wells can be normal depending on design.
  • Built-in dishwashers require stable installation and proper water, drain, and electrical connections for a meaningful test.
  • Internal electrical, pump, heater, and plumbing repairs require model-specific qualified service.
