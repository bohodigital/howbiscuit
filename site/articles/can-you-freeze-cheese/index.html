Can You Freeze Cheese? What Freezes Well and What Changes Texture

Many firm and semi-firm cheeses can be frozen, especially for later cooking. Thawed cheese may be drier, more crumbly, grainy, or separated, and fresh high-moisture varieties generally change the most.

What to know

  • Firm cheese usually tolerates freezing better than fresh cheese.
  • Portion or shred cheese before freezing.
  • Use airtight freezer packaging and thaw in the refrigerator.
  • Texture changes do not automatically mean the cheese became unsafe.

Many cheeses can be frozen, but they do not all return with the same texture.

Firm and semi-firm cheese generally handles freezing better than fresh, soft, or high-moisture cheese. Even when it remains safe, thawed cheese may become dry, crumbly, grainy, separated, or less pleasant to eat by itself.

What to know

  • Firm cheese usually freezes better than fresh cheese.
  • Portioning before freezing makes later use easier.
  • Airtight packaging reduces dehydration and freezer odor.
  • Thaw cheese in the refrigerator.
  • Plan to use texture-changed cheese in cooked dishes.

Cheese that usually freezes more successfully

Cheddar, Monterey Jack, low-moisture mozzarella, Swiss-style cheese, provolone, processed cheese, and other firm varieties are generally more useful after freezing than cottage cheese, ricotta, or cream cheese.

After thawing, firm cheese may work well in casseroles, sauces, soups, pizza, grilled sandwiches, baked pasta, and other dishes where melting or mixing matters more than an unchanged slicing texture.

The National Center for Home Food Preservation notes that cheese can be frozen, while also warning that some varieties become crumbly or mealy. [1]

Cheese that changes the most

Expect larger quality losses from cottage cheese, ricotta, cream cheese, very fresh mozzarella, soft-ripened cheese, high-moisture goat cheese, and delicate spreads or sauces.

These foods can separate into watery and solid components. Some remain useful in a cooked recipe after stirring or blending, but they may be disappointing as a plain spread or filling.

The word “cheese” covers products ranging from hard Parmesan to spoonable ricotta, which is why one freezer rule for the entire category would be convenient, elegant, and wrong.

Portion before freezing

Freeze only an amount that can be used after thawing.

Practical options include recipe-sized blocks, individual slices separated by parchment, shredded portions, and measured quantities for sauces or casseroles.

Smaller packages freeze and thaw more quickly. They also reduce the temptation to thaw an entire block, remove one serving, and refreeze the remainder several times until texture becomes a historical memory.

Package against air

Use freezer-grade bags, airtight freezer containers, or tightly wrapped portions placed inside an outer freezer bag.

Remove excess air without crushing the cheese. Label each package with the cheese type, quantity, freezing date, and intended recipe when helpful.

Thin store wrap may be adequate for a brief period but often provides poor protection against dehydration and freezer odor during longer storage.

Keep the freezer cold

Maintain the freezer at 0°F or below. A freezer thermometer verifies the actual temperature more reliably than the control dial.

Food held continuously frozen can remain safe beyond a recommended quality window, while flavor and texture continue to decline. Treat storage recommendations as planning guidance rather than a magical instant-expiration clock.

How to thaw cheese

Thaw the package in the refrigerator and keep it wrapped while thawing. This limits surface drying and keeps the food cold throughout the process.

Shredded cheese can often be added directly from frozen to pizza, casseroles, soup, or sauce. A solid block should usually thaw before slicing or grating because a frozen block is awkward to handle safely.

Do not pry apart frozen cheese with a knife while holding it in your hand. Emergency departments already receive enough creative submissions.

Can cheese be refrozen?

Cheese that thawed under refrigeration and remained within safe temperature limits may be refrozen, but another freeze-thaw cycle usually causes further quality loss.

Freeze small portions initially so refreezing is unnecessary. Discard cheese with an unsafe temperature history, contamination, unintended mold on a soft variety, or signs of spoilage.

Bottom line

Freeze firm cheese in small airtight portions and expect it to be most useful for cooking after thawing. Fresh and high-moisture cheeses are more likely to separate or become grainy, so freeze them only when that texture change is acceptable.

Limits and cautions

  • Results vary with cheese composition, packaging, and storage conditions.
  • Recommended storage periods describe quality and assume continuous safe freezer temperature.
