How to Choose the Right Size Dehumidifier

Match capacity in pints per day to both the space and its untreated moisture condition. A wet basement needs more capacity than a mildly damp bedroom of the same floor area. Then compare efficiency, low-temperature operation, drainage, noise, filter access, and recall status.

Updated Aug 3, 2026 4 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Room size alone does not determine dehumidifier capacity; baseline humidity and active moisture sources matter.
  • Current capacity ratings should be compared under the same DOE test procedure.
  • Higher Integrated Energy Factor generally means greater efficiency among units of suitable capacity.
  • Choose bucket, gravity drain, or pump operation based on the actual discharge route.

The direct answer

Choose a dehumidifier by matching two things:

  1. The size of the space.
  2. How damp that space becomes without dehumidification.

Capacity is measured in pints of water removed per 24 hours under standardized test conditions.

ENERGY STAR’s current starting ranges are:

| Untreated condition | Below 2,000 sq. ft. | Above 2,000 sq. ft. | |---|---:|---:| | Slightly to moderately damp | 20–30 pints/day | 30+ pints/day | | Very damp | 25–40 pints/day | 40+ pints/day | | Wet or experiencing seepage | 30–50 pints/day | 50+ pints/day |

[1]

These are starting ranges, not guarantees for every building.

Measure the actual humidity

Use a hygrometer and record relative humidity over several days.

Measure in the morning, afternoon, after showers or laundry, during rain, and with doors open and closed. Also note musty odor, condensation, wet flooring, wall dampness, seepage, and seasonal changes.

ENERGY STAR describes 30% to 50% relative humidity as a generally desirable indoor range and warns that higher humidity can encourage mold growth. [1]

A dehumidifier cannot repair a leak, failed drainage system, or missing exhaust ventilation.

Understand pints per day

The advertised capacity is not the bucket size.

A 50-pint unit may have a bucket much smaller than 50 pints because the rating represents moisture removed over 24 hours at prescribed test conditions.

Actual removal depends on room temperature, starting humidity, airflow, door and window openings, moisture sources, coil cleanliness, defrost operation, setpoint, and placement.

Compare current ratings with current ratings

Test conditions and capacity labels have changed over time.

An older unit displaying a larger historical pint rating may not be directly comparable with a current DOE-rated product. ENERGY STAR warns shoppers that capacity options changed when the federal test procedure changed. [1]

Use the current certification record and current pints-per-day value.

Mildly damp rooms

A space is mildly or moderately damp when it sometimes feels humid or smells musty but has no standing water or persistent wall and floor wetness.

For a space below 2,000 square feet, ENERGY STAR starts around 20 to 30 pints per day. Smaller capacity may be adequate when the room is enclosed and moisture sources are controlled.

Very damp or wet basements

More capacity is appropriate when damp odor is constant, walls or floors show wet spots, relative humidity commonly exceeds 75%, laundry dries in the room, groundwater seepage occurs, or large open areas connect to the basement.

A wet basement also needs source control such as gutters, grading, drainage, plumbing repair, a sump system, or foundation repair.

Running a larger appliance beneath an active leak produces a larger electricity bill and the same leak.

Check operating temperature

Refrigerant dehumidifiers become less effective in cold rooms because frost can form on their coils.

ENERGY STAR advises checking low-temperature capability when a space regularly falls below 65°F. Some models provide automatic defrost or low-temperature operation. [1]

Record the room temperature during the season of concern.

Compare efficiency

Dehumidifier efficiency is reported as Integrated Energy Factor, measured in liters of water removed per kilowatt-hour.

Higher IEF generally means greater efficiency when comparing units that are otherwise suitable for the same moisture load. ENERGY STAR publishes the current efficiency criteria for certified products. [2]

Do not choose an efficient unit too small for the space.

Choose the drainage method

Bucket

Useful when no drain is nearby and emptying is manageable. Check bucket capacity, handle, fill indicator, automatic shutoff, and spill control.

Gravity hose

Useful when a floor drain or sump is lower than the outlet and the hose can descend continuously without kinks or freezing. Gravity cannot send water uphill.

Built-in pump

Useful when condensate must reach a sink, window, or higher drain. Check maximum lift, hose requirements, pump warranty, and what happens after pump failure.

ENERGY STAR identifies bucket, gravity drain, and condensate-pump arrangements as major selection factors. [1]

Compare noise and placement

Review sound information at low fan, high fan, compressor operation, pump operation, and defrost.

Confirm the air intake, discharge direction, wall clearance, filter access, bucket-removal path, hose route, grounded outlet, cord length, and floor stability.

Do not route drainage where it can reach a cord, outlet, or appliance.

Check recalls

Dehumidifiers have been subject to major fire-hazard recalls. Search the exact manufacturer, model, and date code in the CPSC database before buying, especially when the product is used or old stock.

A recalled unit should not be used or resold until the official remedy is completed.

Bottom line

Choose capacity from both floor area and untreated moisture conditions.

Then compare the current DOE rating, Integrated Energy Factor, low-temperature operation, drainage, noise, filter access, and recall status. Fix major water sources before expecting a portable appliance to manage them indefinitely.

Limits and cautions

  • Published capacity is measured under standardized conditions and will differ from household water removal.
  • A dehumidifier cannot repair plumbing leaks, foundation seepage, failed gutters, or missing exhaust ventilation.
  • Sizing ranges are starting points rather than performance guarantees for every building.
