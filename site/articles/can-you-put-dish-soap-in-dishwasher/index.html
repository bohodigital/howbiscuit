Can You Put Dish Soap in a Dishwasher?

No. Ordinary hand dish soap can create excessive suds and leaks in an automatic dishwasher; use detergent labeled for automatic dishwashers.

Updated Aug 1, 2026 3 min read Researched Not hands-on tested
Disclosure

This article contains no paid or affiliate links.

Read the disclosure

What to know

  • Hand dish soap and automatic-dishwasher detergent are not interchangeable.
  • If suds appear, stop the cycle and follow the machine manual’s cancel/drain guidance.
  • Keep people away from a slippery leak and disconnect power only by a safe, accessible method described by the manufacturer.

On this page

  1. If you added soap but have not started the cycle
  2. If the machine is already full of suds
  3. Why the products behave differently
  4. Before running the next load
  5. Limits of this guidance

Do not put ordinary hand dish soap in an automatic dishwasher. Hand-washing liquid is designed to foam as you agitate it. Inside a dishwasher, spraying action can create a mass of suds that escapes the tub, interferes with operation, and leaves residue. Use only detergent labeled for automatic dishwashers. [1]

The diagram is both a prevention rule and an accident response: do not substitute, stop when you discover the mistake, and return to the specified product after cleanup.

If you added soap but have not started the cycle

Do not start it “just to see.” Remove the loaded dishes so they do not obstruct cleanup. Scoop or blot out as much soap as practical without pushing it deeper into openings. Consult the manual for safe access to the tub and for the correct cancel/drain procedure. Avoid mixing in another household chemical; an improvised reaction can create more residue or a new hazard.

If the detergent cup is removable only through disassembly, stop and get model-specific support. A simple mistake is not a reason to work near hidden electrical parts.

If the machine is already full of suds

Press the model’s cancel or cancel/drain control. If foam or water is escaping, keep children and pets away and address the slip hazard. Use towels to contain water without reaching under a powered, leaking appliance. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for safely shutting off power when needed; the safe method depends on the installation.

Whirlpool’s consumer guidance recommends stopping the cycle and draining the dishwasher after accidental hand soap use. [1] The number of drain or rinse cycles required depends on how much soap entered and how the machine handles water. Do not add more automatic detergent until the foam and residue are gone.

Some online remedies tell people to add cooking oil, fabric softener, salt, vinegar, or another product to knock down foam. This article does not endorse them because the declared manufacturer guidance instead directs owners to stop the cycle, drain, and use the model’s cleanup procedure. [1] Use the manual or the brand’s support channel for the exact model.

Why the products behave differently

Automatic-dishwasher detergent is formulated for a closed spray system with controlled foaming. Hand dish liquid is formulated for a sink, where visible suds are acceptable and easy to rinse away. The fact that both clean dishes does not make their formulations interchangeable.

A Whirlpool use-and-care guide instructs owners to use automatic dishwashing detergent and separately describes the machine’s detergent dispenser. [2] Your manual is the authoritative reference for compatible product form, amount, dispenser placement, water-hardness adjustments, and cycle choice.

Before running the next load

Inspect the floor and toe-kick area for remaining water, make sure the tub no longer contains foam, and verify that the machine drains normally using the approved controls. If it leaks, displays an error, will not drain, smells electrical, or behaves abnormally, stop and arrange service. Water can reach areas that are not visible from the tub.

Store hand soap and dishwasher detergent in clearly different locations or containers, while keeping both secured from children. Never transfer a cleaning product into an unlabeled food or drink container.

Limits of this guidance

This article is general, source-based guidance and does not assess whether soap reached a pump, sensor, insulation, or electrical component. It reports no cleanup trial. Model-specific instructions take priority, and a persistent leak or electrical symptom requires qualified support rather than repeated trial cycles.

Limits and cautions

  • Cleanup and drain controls differ by dishwasher model.
  • This article does not recommend improvised chemical or cooking-oil remedies.
  • No detergent or dishwasher was evaluated for this article.
